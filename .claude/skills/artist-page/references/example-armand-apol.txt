ABOUT ARMAND APOL
========================================
Armand Apol was born in Brussels in 1879 and died there in 1950. He trained at the Brussels Academy of Fine Arts from 1891 to 1901, where his teachers included the painter Constant Montald.

In 1901 he joined Le Sillon, an artists' society whose members included Willem Paerels, Emile Thysebaert and Arthur Navez. He found his subjects in old Flemish towns such as Bruges, Mechelen and Lissewege, along rivers and canals, and on the North Sea coast and its ports. His early salon entries, with titles like Old Barges, Winter Twilight and Grey Weather on the Scheldt, show how early these themes took hold.

During the First World War, Apol was held as a prisoner of war in Germany. Afterwards he lived for a time in Switzerland and had a solo exhibition at the Galerie Moos in Geneva in 1918.

His career began with struggle, but through group salons and solo exhibitions he became successful enough to make a reasonable living from his art. Alongside painting he made etchings and lithographs, including prints after the work of Valerius De Saedeleer. Collections in Brussels, Ixelles, Ghent, Liège, Verviers and Geneva hold his work.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Apol painted in an impressionist-realist manner: real places, observed closely, but with an eye for light and weather. Water is everywhere in his work. Canals reflect brick houses, barges sit at their moorings, sluice gates hold back still ponds, and fishing boats crowd harbour quays. He moved easily between sunlit scenes, picturesque old streets and stormy seas, and he painted many winter landscapes.

The collection also shows a warmer, southern side. Works dated 1929 picture Marseille's Old Port at sunset, the harbour at Cassis, the viaduct at Vence and the hills of Cagnes. A group from 1925 turns indoors to bathers, seated women and dancers, and there are still lifes of fruit, fish and flowers.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Armand Apol part of?
A: Apol is associated with Post-Impressionism, and his landscapes are described as impressionist-realist. He was also a member of Le Sillon, an artists' society he joined in 1901.

Q: What is Armand Apol known for?
A: He is best known for landscapes, old town corners and seascapes, especially Belgian canals, barges, harbours and fishing boats. He also painted many winter landscapes and made etchings and lithographs.

Q: Where can I see Armand Apol's original works?
A: His work is held in collections in Belgium and Switzerland, including the Print Room of the Royal Library of Belgium in Brussels, the Museum of Ixelles, and the Museum of Fine Arts in Ghent. Collections in Liège, Verviers and Geneva also hold his work.

Q: What makes Armand Apol's style recognizable?
A: Look for water: canals, ponds, sluice gates and harbours, painted with attention to changing light and weather. His scenes are grounded in real places, from Bruges and Mechelen to Marseille's Old Port.

Q: Is Armand Apol the same artist as Louis Apol?
A: No. Louis Apol was a painter from The Hague known for winter forest scenes, while Armand Apol was a Belgian artist from Brussels. Both painted winter landscapes, which is why the names are sometimes mixed up.
