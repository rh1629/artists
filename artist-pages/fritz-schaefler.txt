========================================
ABOUT FRITZ SCHAEFLER
========================================
Fritz Schaefler was born on 31 December 1888 in Eschau, a village in the Spessart hills of Lower Franconia, and died in Cologne in 1954. He began studying architecture in Munich in 1905, moved to the School of Applied Arts in 1906, and entered the Munich Academy of Fine Arts in 1908, where he studied under Angelo Jank. His early paintings were naturalistic, in a late-Impressionist manner, and were shown at the Munich and Berlin Secessions.

In 1914 he was called up and sent to the Western Front. He was badly wounded in the head at the Battle of the Somme in 1916 and left the army in 1917. Back in Munich, his war experiences led to a new phase of dramatic Expressionist woodcuts and etchings. In 1918 and 1919 he took part in the Munich Revolution, served as secretary of the Action Committee of Revolutionary Artists, and made woodcuts for the revolutionary magazine Der Weg. Friends from these years included Georg Schrimpf, Paul Klee and Erich Heckel. When the revolution was crushed in 1919, he took refuge with the artist Alfred Kubin.

In 1920 Schaefler settled with his family at Prien am Chiemsee, where he painted vivid landscapes and figure scenes in watercolour and egg tempera. In 1923 he travelled to Italy with the art historian Kurt Gerstenberg, who helped place his work with collectors. One of them, the Cologne textile manufacturer Joseph Heymann, became his patron, and in 1927 Schaefler moved to Cologne. There he became friends with the city's progressive artists, including Heinrich Hoerle and Franz Wilhelm Seiwert, designed colour schemes for housing estates and hospitals, and in the 1930s made more than a hundred stained-glass windows, altarpieces and sgraffiti.

Under the Nazis his career suffered badly. In 1937 his works were confiscated from German museums, including the Wallraf-Richartz-Museum in Cologne and the Museum Folkwang in Essen, and shown in the Degenerate Art exhibition. The Heymann family emigrated to England that year, and around 80 of his works survived the war in their collection. After 1945 he lived quietly in Cologne, where he died on 24 April 1954. Interest revived with a 1972 Munich exhibition that called him an unknown Expressionist, and his work is now held by LACMA in Los Angeles, MoMA in New York, the British Museum in London and the Hamburger Kunsthalle.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Schaefler is counted among the German Expressionists. His work is marked by strong, often energetic brushwork and intense colour, and his subjects range from portraits and landscapes to scenes that comment on the social and political events of his time. As a printmaker he worked in woodcut, etching and drypoint. His revolution-era prints include a woodcut portrait of Kurt Eisner, and a series called Die Meister (The Masters) includes a 1920 drypoint of the art historian Heinrich Wölfflin. In Cologne his outlines became firmer, and he added city views and scenes of urban life.

The collection centres on his most colourful years, from 1918 to 1923. Most of the prints date from 1920, the year he settled at Lake Chiemsee: landscapes built from angular planes of yellow, blue and green, a mountain village with a church spire, sailing boats under rays of evening light, and flowers in a blue vase. Kleinstadtstraße, from 1918, shows a tilting town street, and Wölfflin I, from 1923, is a black-and-white portrait.

DECORATING WITH SCHAEFLER PRINTS
========================================
Schaefler's saturated colour gives a room an instant lift. Boote in der Abendsonne, with its yellows and blues, works well above a sofa or in a coastal-style living room, while Blumen in blauer Vase brings bright orange and turquoise to a kitchen or dining room. Both suit spaces that get plenty of daylight.

His 1920 landscapes, such as Bergdorf, Kubistische Landschaft and Moorlandschaft, share a palette of deep blue, green and yellow, so they hang well together as a pair or a small group on a gallery wall, in a study, bedroom or hallway. For contrast, add the black-and-white Wölfflin I to a study or library wall alongside the colour works.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Fritz Schaefler part of?
A: Schaefler was a German Expressionist painter and printmaker. In 1918 and 1919 he was active in the Munich Revolution's Action Committee of Revolutionary Artists and made woodcuts for the magazine Der Weg.

Q: What is Fritz Schaefler known for?
A: He is known for dramatic Expressionist prints from the war and revolution years, including portraits, and for colourful landscapes and figure scenes painted at Lake Chiemsee from 1920. In Cologne he also made stained glass, altarpieces and colour designs for buildings.

Q: Where can I see Fritz Schaefler's original works?
A: His work is held by LACMA in Los Angeles, the Museum of Modern Art in New York, the British Museum in London and the Hamburger Kunsthalle. LACMA's collection includes his woodcut Portrait of Kurt Eisner.

Q: What makes Fritz Schaefler's style recognizable?
A: Look for intense colour and energetic brushwork, with landscapes broken into angular planes of yellow, blue and green. His prints use sharp, expressive black lines.

Q: Is Fritz Schaefler the same artist as Fritz Schäfler?
A: Yes. Schäfler is a spelling of the same name with an umlaut, and some catalogues list him that way. Both refer to the Expressionist painter born in Eschau in 1888.
