ABOUT ALBRECHT DE VRIENDT
========================================
Albrecht Frans Lieven De Vriendt was born in Ghent on 8 December 1843 and died in Antwerp on 14 October 1900. His father, Jan Bernard De Vriendt, was a decorative painter who gave him his first training, and Albrecht helped him on decorative work. He showed his first paintings in 1861. His brother Juliaan also became a history painter, and the two were close friends.

In 1865 he moved to Antwerp, where he studied under Victor Lagye and came under the influence of Henri Leys, the leading painter of scenes from Flemish history. He later settled in Brussels. With Juliaan he travelled to Germany in 1869, and in 1880 to Italy and then to Egypt and Palestine, a journey that produced studies, watercolours and pastels. Early successes included Jacqueline of Bavaria Imploring Philip the Good to Pardon Her Husband (1871), now in Liège, and Philip I Conferring the Order of the Golden Fleece on His Son Charles (1880), painted for the 50th anniversary of Belgian independence.

In 1891 he succeeded Charles Verlat as director of the Royal Academy of Fine Arts in Antwerp, a post he held until his death. In 1894 he also became a professor at the National Higher Institute for Fine Arts in Antwerp and was made a commander of the Order of Leopold. His students included Frans Mortelmans and Isidoor Opsomer.

His largest commission was the decoration of the Gothic Hall in Bruges City Hall, which was created from two older council chambers between 1895 and 1905. He planned a cycle of scenes from the city's history, from Bruges receiving its charter to the first chapter of the Order of the Golden Fleece. In 1894 a Flemish art magazine already counted his designs among the best of their kind. He died before the murals were finished, and Juliaan completed them with the help of Samuel De Vriendt. The Royal Museum of Fine Arts Antwerp (KMSKA) holds his oil sketches for the murals, a series of 35 historical figures from 1889, and Pope Paul III in Front of the Portrait of Luther (1883). Other works are in Liège, Brussels, the Groeningemuseum in Bruges and the Brooklyn Museum in New York.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
De Vriendt belonged to the Romantic-historical school of Belgian painting and, like Leys, looked back to Flanders in the 15th to 17th centuries. His pictures are carefully researched costume pieces: armour, banners, patterned fabrics and Gothic interiors, drawn with clear outlines and painted in strong reds, blues and golds. Alongside his public commissions he painted smaller scenes of court life, such as a game of chess or a queen with her lady-in-waiting. His work and Juliaan's show strong similarities.

The collection shows both sides of his work. A long series of standing figures, each set against a gold ground, presents counts and countesses of Flanders, saints, knights, abbots, architects and the painters Jan van Eyck, Hans Memling and Gerard David. Crowded scenes from Bruges history show the triumphant return of the city's citizens from the Battle of the Golden Spurs, an annual fair in a timber market hall and bread being handed out to the poor. Three further paintings, including His Move, show his quieter interiors.

DECORATING WITH DE VRIENDT PRINTS
========================================
The standing figures, with their gold grounds and strong reds and blues, work best in sets. Their tall, narrow shape suits a hallway, a staircase wall or the spaces either side of a fireplace or door. Hang three or four in a row, or pair a countess with a count, such as Judith, Wife of Baldwin I of Flanders with Baldwin I of Flanders, for a formal, almost heraldic look.

The crowded Bruges scenes need more room. A wide picture such as The Annual Fair makes a focal point above a sofa, sideboard or dining table, especially with warm wood and deep colours around it. For a study, library or reading corner, His Move, with its game of chess, brings a quiet, thoughtful note.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Albrecht De Vriendt part of?
A: He belonged to the Belgian Romantic-historical school of history painting and continued the tradition of Henri Leys. He also took part in Belgian monumentalism, the movement for large public murals that the Belgian state promoted from the mid-1850s.

Q: What is Albrecht De Vriendt known for?
A: He is best known for scenes from Flemish and Belgian history, above all the history murals he began in the Gothic Hall of Bruges City Hall. He was also director of the Royal Academy of Fine Arts in Antwerp from 1891 until his death in 1900.

Q: Where can I see Albrecht De Vriendt's original works?
A: The murals he began can be seen in the Gothic Hall of Bruges City Hall. The Royal Museum of Fine Arts Antwerp (KMSKA) holds his oil sketches for them and his 35 historical figures of 1889, and other works are in the Fine Arts Museum in Liège, the Groeningemuseum in Bruges and the Brooklyn Museum in New York.

Q: How can I recognise Albrecht De Vriendt's style?
A: Look for carefully researched medieval costume, armour, banners and Gothic interiors, with clear outlines and rich reds, blues and gold. His single figures often stand against a plain gold ground.

Q: Are Albrecht De Vriendt and Albert De Vriendt the same artist?
A: Yes. His full name was Albrecht Frans Lieven De Vriendt, and French-language sources call him Albert. He should not be confused with his brother Juliaan De Vriendt, also a history painter, who succeeded him as director of the Antwerp Academy.
