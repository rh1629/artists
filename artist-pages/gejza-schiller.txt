========================================
ABOUT GEJZA SCHILLER
========================================
Gejza Schiller, also known by the Hungarian form of his name, Géza Schiller, was a Hungarian painter, graphic artist and illustrator. He was born in the mid-1890s. He probably studied painting in Budapest and then in Berlin, where he moved after the fall of the Hungarian Republic.

In the early 1920s he came to Košice, in what is now eastern Slovakia, at the invitation of Josef Polák, director of the East Slovak Museum. In those years Košice was one of the most important centres of modern art in the region, and Schiller became a leading figure of the movement later called Košice Modernism. He was a close friend of the painter František Foltýn. He exhibited in Uzhhorod in 1922, and in 1923 he and Foltýn showed their work together at the East Slovak Museum.

In 1924 he left Košice for Transylvania, where he turned increasingly to writing and art theory. He is also recorded as working in Arad, in present-day Romania. He died young, in 1928. Today the East Slovak Gallery in Košice holds a group of his paintings and drawings from the early 1920s.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Schiller's subjects were mostly scenes of town life, some with a social edge, along with landscapes and country work. His forms are simplified and shaped by Cubism, with firm outlines, and his figures sometimes have a touch of caricature. His method has been described as a Cézannesque constructivism. Bright colour and carefully built compositions give his scenes a still, calm mood.

The collection covers the main sides of his Košice years. There are crowded town and café scenes such as Mestský motív and V kaviarni; landscapes like Krajina s mostom, with its arched bridge, and Záber z Levoče, a view in the town of Levoča; and country scenes of farm work and harvest in Hospodársky dvor, Oberačka and Z práce. Alongside the oils are portrait heads, a watercolour and several drawings in ink and charcoal, including the angular Dedina and Hlava muža.

========================================
DECORATING WITH SCHILLER PRINTS
========================================
Schiller's paintings are full of deep greens, blues and warm reds, which makes them easy to live with in a living room or kitchen. Mestský motív, with its figures at a riverside railing and red factory roofs, makes a lively focal point above a sofa or dining table, and V kaviarni, a busy café interior, is a natural fit for a kitchen, dining area or home bar.

For a calmer room, the green hills and arched bridge of Krajina s mostom suit a bedroom or study. The ink and charcoal drawings, such as Hlava muža or Štúdia sediaceho muža, work well in simple black frames on a hallway or office wall, and look good hung as a pair.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Gejza Schiller part of?
A: He is considered one of the leading representatives of Košice Modernism, the avant-garde circle that made Košice an important centre of modern art in the 1920s. His style draws on Cubism, and his method has been described as Cézannesque constructivism.

Q: What is Gejza Schiller known for?
A: He is known for scenes of town life, some with a social edge, painted with simplified, Cubist-influenced forms, firm outlines and bright colour. He also painted landscapes and country scenes, and made drawings and illustrations.

Q: Where can I see Gejza Schiller's original works?
A: The East Slovak Gallery (Východoslovenská galéria) in Košice holds a group of his paintings and drawings, including Mestský motív and Krajina s mostom. Its collection can be browsed online through the Slovak art database Web umenia.

Q: Is Gejza Schiller the same artist as Géza Schiller?
A: Yes. Gejza is the Slovak form and Géza the Hungarian form of the same first name, and catalogues use both for him.

Q: How can I recognise Gejza Schiller's style?
A: Look for strong outlines, simplified and slightly angular figures and buildings, and clear blocks of green, blue and red. His scenes, even busy ones, have a still, settled mood.
