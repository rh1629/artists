ABOUT JAN REMBOWSKI
========================================
Jan Rembowski was born in Warsaw in 1879. His father, Aleksy, had taken part in the January Uprising and leased land at Wawrzyszew. Jan took his first drawing lessons from the painter Wojciech Gerson, then studied sculpture at the Kraków Academy of Fine Arts under Konstanty Laszczka. From 1905 to 1907 he studied painting with Józef Mehoffer.

In 1905 he helped found the Group of Five, also called the Norwid Group after the poet Cyprian Norwid, with Leopold Gottlieb, Wlastimil Hofman, Mieczysław Jakimowicz and Witold Wojtkiewicz. The group believed that literature, music and the visual arts belonged together. The same year he married Marie Julaine, a French teacher, in Zakopane. They had two daughters: Janina, who died as a child in 1910, and Hanna, who became an illustrator.

Rembowski had tuberculosis, which often took him to Zakopane for his health and in time forced him to give up sculpture. He was a member of the Sztuka Podhalańska (Podhale Art) society, founded in Zakopane, and of the Polish Applied Art Society. In 1909 and 1910 he worked on the interior of Bronisława and Kazimierz Dłuski's sanatorium in Zakopane, painting decorative panels including Procession of Highlanders. He also wrote poetry and essays on Podhale folk culture, and illustrated the first edition of Marian Falski's Elementarz (1910), a popular Polish alphabet book.

During the First World War he served in the Polish Legions, despite his illness. He exhibited widely until 1920 and died in Warsaw in 1923, aged 44. He is buried with his daughter Hanna at Powązki Cemetery. Most of his work was destroyed during the Second World War, and only a small part survives. The National Museum in Warsaw holds a group of his pastels and paintings, and the Museum of Art in Łódź holds his oil Zaczarowana dzieweczka II (1913).

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Rembowski worked in oil, pastel, sculpture and printmaking, and also designed bookplates, interiors and everyday objects. His work belongs to Symbolism and Art Nouveau. Stanisław Wyspiański was a key influence, seen in Rembowski's decorative handling of the flat picture surface, his strong outlines, and the wilting or bowed plants that appear in his melancholy portraits. Highland culture fascinated him, and many of his works draw on Tatra traditions and legends.

The collection reflects this range. Highlanders appear again and again, from pencil heads and seated mountaineers to the 1909 processions of Highland men, women and children in cloaks and shawls. There are portraits of his wife and mother, several self-portraits, and the pastel allegories of Summer and Winter: a young woman crowned with flowers, and an old man crowned with dry leaves. Symbolic dragon scenes, religious subjects, wartime works such as Invalid – veteran (1917), chrysanthemums, landscapes and windows of flower pots complete the picture.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Jan Rembowski part of?
A: Rembowski was a Symbolist, and his work is also associated with Art Nouveau. In 1905 he helped found the Group of Five, or Norwid Group, which linked painting with literature and music.

Q: What is Jan Rembowski known for?
A: He is known for paintings and pastels of Highlanders and Podhale folk culture, Symbolist allegories and melancholy portraits. He also illustrated the first edition of Marian Falski's Elementarz, a popular Polish alphabet book.

Q: Where can I see Jan Rembowski's original works?
A: The National Museum in Warsaw holds a group of his pastels and paintings, including Return of Tobias (1919) and Portrait of a Peasant (1922), and the Museum of Art in Łódź holds Zaczarowana dzieweczka II (1913). In 2022–2023 the National Museum in Kraków showed his panel Procession of Highlanders at its Villa Atma branch in Zakopane.

Q: How can I recognise Jan Rembowski's style?
A: Look for strong outlines and a flat, decorative picture surface, often in pastel. His figures are frequently Highlanders in folk dress, and his portraits often include drooping or bowed plants, a touch linked to Stanisław Wyspiański.

Q: Is Jan Rembowski related to Hanna Rembowska?
A: Yes. Hanna Rembowska (1912–1947), an illustrator, was his daughter, and they are buried together at Powązki Cemetery in Warsaw.
