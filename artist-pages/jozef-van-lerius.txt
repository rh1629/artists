========================================
ABOUT JOZEF VAN LERIUS
========================================
Jozef Van Lerius was born in Boom, near Antwerp, in 1823, and died in Mechelen in 1876. His full name was Joseph Henri François Van Lerius, so you will see him listed as both Jozef and Joseph. By 1838, still in his teens, he was studying drawing at the Brussels Academy. From 1839 to 1844 he trained as a painter in Antwerp under Gustave Wappers.

In 1852 he travelled through Germany and Italy. Two years later he was appointed to teach painting at the Royal Academy of Fine Arts in Antwerp. His pupils there included Lawrence Alma-Tadema, Aloïs Boudry, Gerard Portielje, Henri Van Dyck, Eugène Wolters and Piet Verhaert. He received the Knight's Cross of the Order of Leopold in 1861 and the Bavarian Order of Saint Michael in 1869.

His pictures found buyers well beyond Belgium. Queen Victoria bought his Premier Né (The First Born) in 1852, and it is still at Windsor Castle. His Lady Godiva was shown at the Antwerp Triennial Salon in 1870 and bought by the London dealer Henry Graves. He worked with the Antwerp dealer Albert D'Huyvetter, who had a business in New York, to sell in the United States, and other paintings went to San Francisco and St Petersburg. Engravings of Lady Godiva and Cinderella spread his best-known images widely.

In 1875 he fell ill with meningitis, and he died the following year in Mechelen, where he had gone for treatment. Karel (Charles) Verlat succeeded him as a teacher at the Academy. Van Lerius was buried with national honours at the Kiel cemetery in Antwerp and later moved to Schoonselhof. Museums in Antwerp, Brussels, Amsterdam, Freiburg, Coventry and Washington hold his work.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Van Lerius was part of the Antwerp Romantic movement. He painted mythological and biblical scenes, portraits and genre pictures, and much of his work set out to teach a lesson, as in De Lezing (The Reading), where one girl reads while another listens. Fairy tales and legends suited him: Cinderella toiling in the kitchen while her stepsisters dress for the ball, or Lady Godiva. His finish is smooth and careful, with close attention to faces, fabric and lace.

The collection shows this range. There are society portraits, such as Princess Herminie de Looz et Corswarem seated beside a large dog, and tender oval portraits of children. The Augustinermuseum in Freiburg notes that he often travelled in Austria, Germany and Italy, painting the countryside and its people, and the collection has young women in regional dress from Dalarna in Sweden (1862) and the Hotzenwald in the Black Forest. Myth, legend and literature appear in The Rape of Europe, Esmeralda and Djali and Jeanne d'Arc au siège de Paris, alongside a quiet landscape, Schwalbach Nassau (1868), and a self-portrait.

DECORATING WITH VAN LERIUS PRINTS
========================================
Van Lerius's portraits, with their dark backgrounds and soft skin tones, suit a hallway, study or formal living room, where a single portrait or a pair can hold a wall. His oval paintings of children, such as Two Children Playing Around and Madame Valentine De Give-LeDelier as a Child, work well in a nursery, a child's bedroom or on a family landing.

For more colour, the costume portraits stand out: the red bodice of Young Girl from the Hotzenwald or the sheepskin jacket and red tassels of Girl from Dalarne bring warmth to a dining room or entrance hall. Cinderella, with its hearth, doves and tiled floor, is a natural choice for a kitchen, and mythological scenes such as Idyll and The Rape of Europe suit a bedroom or a quiet reading room.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Jozef Van Lerius part of?
A: He belonged to Antwerp Romanticism and painted in the Romantic-historical style. He trained under Gustave Wappers and later taught painting at the Antwerp Academy.

Q: What is Jozef Van Lerius known for?
A: He is best known for Lady Godiva, shown at the Antwerp Triennial Salon in 1870, and for his Cinderella, both widely reproduced as engravings. He also painted portraits, genre scenes and mythological and biblical subjects.

Q: Where can I see Jozef Van Lerius's original works?
A: In Belgium, his work is held by the Royal Museum of Fine Arts Antwerp, the Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium in Brussels and the Museum Mayer van den Bergh in Antwerp, which is closed for renovation with reopening planned for 2029. Elsewhere, his work is at Windsor Castle, the Augustinermuseum in Freiburg, the Herbert Art Gallery and Museum in Coventry and the Smithsonian American Art Museum in Washington.

Q: How can I recognise Van Lerius's style?
A: Look for smooth, polished painting, gentle idealised faces and close attention to fabric, lace and costume. Many of his pictures tell a story, from fairy tales and legends to scenes with a moral lesson.

Q: Is Jozef Van Lerius the same artist as Joseph Van Lerius?
A: Yes. His full name was Joseph Henri François Van Lerius, and Jozef is the Dutch form. Both spellings refer to the same painter.
