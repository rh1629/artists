ABOUT HERMANN SCHERER
========================================
Hermann Scherer was born on 8 February 1893 in Rümmingen, a village in Germany's Markgräflerland not far from the Swiss border. He left school in 1907 to train as a stonemason in nearby Lörrach. He then worked for a series of Basel sculptors, Carl Gutknecht, Otto Roos and Carl Burckhardt, and helped Burckhardt with his fountain figures Rhine and Wiese at Basel's Badischer Bahnhof.

His early sculpture followed a calm, classical ideal. In the early 1920s he turned to a modern way of working and took up painting, and he destroyed many of his earlier works. An Edvard Munch exhibition in Zurich made a deep impression on him. In June 1923 he helped Ernst Ludwig Kirchner hang his exhibition at the Kunsthalle Basel, and Kirchner invited him to Davos. On long working visits there, Scherer began to carve figures in wood and to cut woodcuts.

At the turn of 1924 and 1925 he founded the artists' group Rot-Blau (Red-Blue) with Albert Müller and Paul Camenisch, and Werner Neuhaus joined soon after. The group took the German Brücke artists as its model. Its first show, at the Kunsthalle Basel in 1925, caused a stir, and five of Scherer's sculptures were removed from view on moral grounds. In the summer of 1925 he worked in the Mendrisiotto in southern Ticino with Camenisch and Neuhaus. He and Kirchner fell out that year, but Kirchner still helped him show at the International Art Exhibition in Dresden in 1926.

Scherer fell seriously ill in the autumn of 1926 and died in Basel on 13 May 1927, aged 34. In his last two and a half years he made more than a hundred woodcuts and around 25 wooden sculptures. The Kunsthalle Basel held a memorial exhibition of over 200 works in 1928, and the Kunstmuseum Basel gave him a major retrospective in 2022. The Dreiländermuseum in Lörrach holds 118 of his works, mostly woodcuts.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Scherer is one of the best-known Expressionists to work in Switzerland. After 1921 he left behind the muted tones then popular in Basel and painted with bold, clashing colour contrasts. Carving wood changed everything he made: soft, flowing outlines gave way to sharp, angular shapes, and his paintings and drawings took on the same energetic, slashing marks. His woodcuts include portraits of artist friends and dramatic landscapes, but above all they deal with love and desire, togetherness and loneliness, fear and excess.

The collection shows both sides of his work. Stark black-and-white woodcuts such as Atelierfest, Der Fremde, Paar im Wald and Selbstbildnis mit Pinseln sit alongside colour paintings of the Ticino and of Basel, including Villa Loverciana with Blossoming Trees, Landscape with Chestnut Trees and The 'Pfalz' in Basel. Couples and small groups of figures in landscapes come up again and again, and there is also a 1926 exhibition poster for the Kunsthalle Basel.

DECORATING WITH SCHERER PRINTS
========================================
Scherer's colour paintings are full of energy, so give them room. Villa Loverciana with Blossoming Trees or Landscape with Chestnut Trees, with their pinks, violets and greens, will lift a living room or dining room, and they sit well against white or pale grey walls. Mädchen in rotem Kleid makes a strong single piece for a bedroom or landing.

His black-and-white woodcuts suit a study, hallway or modern monochrome interior. Hang Tessiner Landschaft beside the woodcut Villa Loverciana for a pair of mountain views, or pair the woodcut Atelierfest with the painting Party in the Studio to show the same lively subject in black and white and in colour.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Hermann Scherer part of?
A: Scherer was an Expressionist. He co-founded the artists' group Rot-Blau, modelled on the German Brücke group, which made the most important contribution to Expressionism in Switzerland.

Q: What is Hermann Scherer known for?
A: He is best known for his wooden sculptures, which are counted among the most important of Expressionism. He also made more than a hundred woodcuts and painted in bold, contrasting colours.

Q: Where can I see Hermann Scherer's original works?
A: The Dreiländermuseum in Lörrach holds 118 of his works, mostly woodcuts. MASI Lugano holds his Self-Portrait in a Ticino Landscape, and the Pfalzgalerie in Kaiserslautern holds his sculpture Das kleine Mädchen.

Q: How can I recognise Hermann Scherer's style?
A: Look for sharp, angular shapes and strong contrasts: hard black and white in the woodcuts, clashing colours in the paintings. His forms often look carved, even on canvas, because his work in wood shaped how he drew and painted.

Q: Is Hermann Scherer the artist the same person as Hermann Scherer the author?
A: No. Hermann Scherer the painter and sculptor lived from 1893 to 1927. The German author and entrepreneur of the same name was born in 1964.
