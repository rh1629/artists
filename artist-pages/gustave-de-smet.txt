========================================
ABOUT GUSTAVE DE SMET
========================================
Gustave De Smet was born in Ghent on 21 January 1877 and died in Deurle on 8 October 1943. His father, Jules, was a decorative painter and photographer, and Gustave and his younger brother Léon helped him decorate inns, shops and fairground buildings. He trained at the Royal Academy of Fine Arts in Ghent, where his teachers included the animal painter Jean Delvin, though by his own account he preferred to learn from nature. In 1898 he married Augusta Van Hoorebeke.

Around 1906 he became friends with the painters Constant Permeke and Frits Van den Berghe, friendships that lasted for the rest of his life. In 1908 he moved with his wife and son to Sint-Martens-Latem, the artists' village on the River Lys, where Léon was already working. There he came under the influence of Luminism and the painter Emile Claus. With Permeke and Van den Berghe he belonged to the second group of artists associated with Latem. From 1911 he lived in Ghent again.

When the First World War broke out, he fled to the Netherlands with his family and Van den Berghe. From 1914 to 1922 they lived in and around Amsterdam, Laren and Blaricum. The Dutch painter Leo Gestel befriended him, and the work of the Bergen School, the French cubist Henri Le Fauconnier and the German expressionists marked the great turning point in his art. In 1918 his only child, Firmin, was killed at the age of 20 in a train disaster near Weesp. The first book about his work was published in Leipzig in 1923.

He returned to Belgium in 1922, living first with Permeke in Ostend, then at Bachte-Maria-Leerne and Afsnee, before settling in Deurle in 1927. There his mix of Expressionism and Cubism reached its peak, in circus, fairground and village scenes. In 1932 the Brussels gallery Le Centaure, a leading champion of Flemish Expressionism, went bankrupt, at least 100 of his works were auctioned off cheaply, and he had to sell his house. His later home in Deurle is now the Museum Gust De Smet, and his work is held by museums including MSK Ghent, KMSKA in Antwerp, the Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium in Brussels, Mu.ZEE in Ostend and the Kunstmuseum Den Haag.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
De Smet began as an impressionist, painting light and colour in the manner of Emile Claus. In the Netherlands his work changed. He simplified lines and shapes, used strong contrasts of light and dark, and gave his pictures a firm, balanced structure. His palette turned to warm autumn tones and black, laid on in broad brushstrokes. In the mid-1920s his colour cooled for a time and his figures became schematic, fitted together like a puzzle of coloured shapes. Later came the circus, fairground and village scenes for which he is best known. In his own words, he wanted to express inner life "with the greatest possible simplicity, through form and colour".

The collection follows this whole journey. Early works such as Le Béguinage À Bruges and a garden with geese and children are soft and full of light. From the Dutch years come views of the Vondelpark and Amsterdam's rooftops and a woman from the fishing village of Spakenburg. Then there are crowded cafés and village fairs, nudes and women at windows, still lifes of herring and dark flowers, farms and haystacks under heavy skies, and a group of black-and-white prints of reapers, potato diggers, a fishing village and a winter evening.

========================================
DECORATING WITH DE SMET PRINTS
========================================
De Smet's café and fair scenes are sociable pictures, full of people and warm browns, ochres and reds. They suit a dining room, a kitchen or a living room where people gather. Still Life with Herring, with its bottle, bread and fish on a red-brown table, is a natural choice for a kitchen, while A Bunch Of Dark Flowers adds deep, moody colour to a sitting room or bedroom.

For a calmer mood, Dorpsgezicht, a village under an orange sunset, and the farm and haystack landscapes bring quiet countryside colour to a hallway or study. The pale, light-filled Le Béguinage À Bruges suits a bright bedroom. His black-and-white prints, such as The Reaper and Harvesting Potatoes, look striking framed together on a gallery wall, and work well in modern or minimal rooms.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Gustave De Smet part of?
A: De Smet was one of the founders of Flemish Expressionism, together with Constant Permeke and Frits Van den Berghe. Before the First World War these artists formed the second group of painters at Sint-Martens-Latem, and De Smet's work also shows strong cubist influence.

Q: What is Gustave De Smet known for?
A: He is best known for expressionist scenes of Flemish life: village fairs, circus scenes, cafés, farms and villages. He built them from simplified shapes and warm, earthy colour, and he also made prints.

Q: Where can I see Gustave De Smet's original works?
A: His paintings are held by MSK Ghent, KMSKA in Antwerp, the Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium in Brussels and Mu.ZEE in Ostend, among others. His house in Deurle is preserved as the Museum Gust De Smet, and Dutch collections such as the Kunstmuseum Den Haag also hold his work.

Q: What makes Gustave De Smet's style recognizable?
A: Look for simplified, solid figures set into flat planes of colour that fit together like a puzzle, often in warm browns, ochres and black. He often signed his works "Gust. De Smet".

Q: Is Gustave De Smet the same artist as Léon De Smet?
A: No. Léon De Smet was his younger brother, four years his junior, and an impressionist painter. Both brothers trained in Ghent and worked at Sint-Martens-Latem, but Gustave went on to become an expressionist.
