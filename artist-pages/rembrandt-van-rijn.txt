ABOUT REMBRANDT VAN RIJN
========================================
Rembrandt Harmenszoon van Rijn was born in Leiden on 15 July 1606 and died in Amsterdam on 4 October 1669. His father was a miller and his mother a baker's daughter. He went to the Latin school in Leiden and was enrolled at Leiden University in 1620, but painting drew him more. He trained with the Leiden painter Jacob van Swanenburg, then spent six months with the history painter Pieter Lastman in Amsterdam.

In 1625 he set up as an independent painter in Leiden, working closely with his friend Jan Lievens. Gerrit Dou became his first pupil, and the statesman Constantijn Huygens helped him win commissions from the court in The Hague. At the end of 1631 he moved to Amsterdam, where he first lived and worked with the art dealer Hendrick van Uylenburgh and soon became a successful portrait painter. In 1634 he married Uylenburgh's cousin Saskia and joined the city's painters' guild. From 1633 he signed his work simply "Rembrandt".

In 1639 the couple bought a large house in the Breestraat, which is now the Rembrandt House Museum. Three of their children died as babies; only their son Titus, born in 1641, grew up. Saskia died in 1642, the year Rembrandt completed The Night Watch. From 1649 his partner was Hendrickje Stoffels, who had first worked in the house as his maid, and their daughter Cornelia was born in 1654.

Rembrandt spent heavily on art and rare objects, and in 1656 he declared himself insolvent. His collection and house were sold, and the family moved to more modest lodgings on the Rozengracht. Hendrickje and Titus then set up an art-dealing business that employed him, so he could keep working. He still won major commissions, such as The Syndics of 1662, and in these years painted The Jewish Bride and some of his most searching self-portraits. Hendrickje died in 1663 and Titus in 1668. Rembrandt died the following year and was buried in the Westerkerk in Amsterdam.

STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Rembrandt is best known for chiaroscuro: strong contrasts of light and shadow, which he most likely took from Caravaggio's Dutch followers and made his own. Faces are often half lit, with the ridge of the nose marking the line between light and shade. His early paintings are smooth and finely detailed. From the 1650s his colours grew richer and his brushwork rougher, with paint laid on so thickly that its texture suggests form. As an etcher he was just as inventive, using drypoint, films of ink left on the plate and Japanese paper, and his small landscape prints set the course for landscape etching until the end of the 19th century.

Unlike most Dutch painters of his time, he worked across almost every subject, and our collection reflects that range. There are dozens of self-portraits, from wide-eyed early etchings to Rembrandt Laughing, along with portraits of Saskia, Titus and Hendrickje. About a hundred works tell Bible stories, from The Hundred Guilder Print to The Three Crosses. Windmills, cottages, canals and views of Amsterdam fill dozens of landscape etchings and drawings, alongside beggars, street musicians, a sleeping dog and still lifes.

DECORATING WITH REMBRANDT PRINTS
========================================
Rembrandt's etchings, printed in black ink on warm paper, suit calm and light rooms. Landscapes such as The Windmill or Six's Bridge work well in a hallway, study or bedroom, and look good in pairs or a small group in simple frames.

His paintings are darker and richer, with deep browns lit by gold and red. A portrait like Titus van Rijn, the Artist's Son, Reading suits a library or reading corner, while Rembrandt Laughing brings warmth and humour to a living room. Large group scenes such as The Night Watch make a strong focal point above a sofa or fireplace, or in a dining room.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Rembrandt part of?
A: Rembrandt was a leading artist of the Dutch Golden Age, and his work belongs to the Baroque. His use of light and shadow shows the influence of Caravaggio and his Dutch followers, although Rembrandt never travelled to Italy.

Q: What is Rembrandt known for?
A: He is best known for his portraits, self-portraits and Bible scenes, and for his dramatic use of light and shade. He made about 300 paintings, 300 etchings and several hundred drawings, including around 100 self-portraits.

Q: Where can I see Rembrandt's original works?
A: The Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam holds The Night Watch and The Jewish Bride, and the Mauritshuis in The Hague has The Anatomy Lesson of Dr Nicolaes Tulp. The National Gallery in London, the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York and museums in Berlin, Dresden and Kassel also hold large groups of his paintings. His former home in Amsterdam is now the Rembrandt House Museum, which holds almost all of his etchings.

Q: How can I recognise Rembrandt's style?
A: Look for a pool of strong light against deep shadow, often falling across one side of a face. In later works the paint is thick and loosely handled, and people are shown with warmth and feeling rather than stiff formality.

Q: Is The Night Watch set at night?
A: No. The painting shows a civic militia company, and its dark tones come from ageing rather than a night-time setting. The name "The Night Watch" was given later; its formal title is The Militia Company of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq.
