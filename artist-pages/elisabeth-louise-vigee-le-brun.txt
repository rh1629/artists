ABOUT ELISABETH LOUISE VIGÉE LE BRUN
========================================
Elisabeth Louise Vigée Le Brun was born in Paris on 16 April 1755. Her father, Louis Vigée, was a portraitist and pastellist, and he gave her her first lessons before he died when she was twelve. She took advice from the painters Gabriel François Doyen, Jean-Baptiste Greuze and Joseph Vernet, and taught herself by copying old masters such as Rubens, Van Dyck and Rembrandt. By her early teens she was painting portraits professionally, and in 1774 she became a member of the Académie de Saint-Luc.

In 1776 she married Jean-Baptiste-Pierre Le Brun, a painter and art dealer, and in 1780 their daughter Julie was born. Marie Antoinette became her patron, and Vigée Le Brun painted more than 30 portraits of the Queen and her family. A trip to Flanders and the Netherlands in 1781 brought her face to face with the Flemish masters. On 31 May 1783 she was received into the Académie royale de peinture et de sculpture, one of only 15 women granted full membership between 1648 and 1793.

As the French Revolution gathered pace, she fled Paris with her daughter on the night of 5 October 1789. For twelve years she lived and worked abroad: in Italy, Austria, Russia and Germany. She was welcomed by academies in Bologna, Parma, Rome and Saint Petersburg, and in Russia she painted the nobility, including the former King of Poland, Stanisław August Poniatowski. She remained a committed royalist all her life.

She returned to France in January 1802. Later she lived for a time in England and travelled twice in Switzerland, where she painted the writer Madame de Staël. She settled in Louveciennes, near the Seine west of Paris, and in her eighties published her memoirs, Souvenirs. She died in Paris on 30 March 1842, having painted around 660 portraits and 200 landscapes. Her work is held by the Louvre, the Hermitage, the National Gallery in London and the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, among many others.

STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Vigée Le Brun's style is usually placed at the end of the Rococo, with elements of Neoclassicism: Rococo in its subjects and colour, Neoclassical in its simpler lines. She disliked the fussy fashions of her day and often persuaded sitters to change their clothes, draping them in shawls and long scarves inspired by Raphael and Domenichino. Her portraits feel warm and informal. She showed Marie Antoinette in a plain muslin dress, painted herself embracing her daughter, and caused a stir at the Salon of 1787 with a self-portrait smiling so openly that her teeth showed. She also painted sitters in mythological guise, such as Emma Hamilton as a Bacchante and as a Sibyl.

The collection follows her career across Europe. Portraits of Marie Antoinette sit alongside Calonne, Louis XVI's minister of finance, Madame du Barry, her friend the painter Hubert Robert, and her brother and daughter. Many titles name sitters from Russia and Poland, such as Princess Golitsyna, Izabela Lubomirska and Stanisław August Poniatowski dressed as Henry IV. There are figures in mythological guise, including a Bacchante, Flora and Atalanta, a portrait of the Mysorean envoy Muhammad Dervish Khan, whom she painted in 1788, and a mountain landscape, Paysage montagneux.

DECORATING WITH VIGÉE LE BRUN PRINTS
========================================
Vigée Le Brun's portraits suit rooms where you want a sense of company and calm: a hallway, a dining room or the wall above a fireplace. The pale muslin and soft skin tones of a portrait such as Comtesse de la Châtre sit easily with light walls, warm wood and gilt frames. Her portraits of her daughter, such as Julie Le Brun Looking in a Mirror, bring a gentle, personal note to a bedroom or dressing area.

For a sitting room or study, try a small gallery wall of three or four portraits, mixing men and women, or pair a self-portrait with one of her mythological figures. Paysage montagneux offers a quiet change of pace in a study or on a landing, and works well hung beside a portrait.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Elisabeth Louise Vigée Le Brun part of?
A: Her style is generally considered part of the late Rococo, with elements of Neoclassicism. Her subjects and colours are Rococo, while her simpler compositions point towards the Neoclassical style.

Q: What is Vigée Le Brun known for?
A: She is best known as a portrait painter, and above all for her portraits of Marie Antoinette, of whom she painted more than 30. She painted around 660 portraits in total, including many tender self-portraits with her daughter Julie.

Q: Where can I see Vigée Le Brun's original works?
A: Major collections include the Louvre in Paris, the Palace of Versailles, the National Gallery in London, the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York and the Hermitage in Saint Petersburg. The Royal Collection in Britain holds her portrait of Calonne.

Q: How can I recognise a Vigée Le Brun portrait?
A: Look for soft light, relaxed poses and simple clothing: white muslin dresses, shawls and long scarves rather than stiff court costume. Her sitters often seem warm and approachable, and some even smile.

Q: Was Vigée Le Brun related to the painter Charles Le Brun?
A: Only by marriage. Charles Le Brun, first director of the French Academy under Louis XIV, was the great-great-uncle of her husband, the art dealer Jean-Baptiste-Pierre Le Brun.
