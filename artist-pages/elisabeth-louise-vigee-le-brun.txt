ABOUT ELISABETH LOUISE VIGÉE LE BRUN
========================================
Elisabeth Louise Vigée Le Brun was born in Paris on 16 April 1755 and died there on 30 March 1842. Her father, Louis Vigée, was a portraitist and pastellist, and he gave her her first lessons. He died when she was 12. She went on to learn from the advice of established painters, including Gabriel François Doyen, Jean-Baptiste Greuze and Joseph Vernet, and by copying old masters such as Rubens, Van Dyck and Rembrandt.

By her early teens she was painting portraits professionally, and in 1774 she became a member of the Académie de Saint-Luc. In 1776 she married the painter and art dealer Jean-Baptiste-Pierre Le Brun, and their daughter Julie was born in 1780. She won the patronage of Queen Marie Antoinette and painted more than 30 portraits of the queen and her family. In 1783 she was received into the Royal Academy of Painting and Sculpture, on the same day as her rival Adélaïde Labille-Guiard.

As the Revolution gathered pace, she left Paris with Julie on the night of 5 October 1789. For 12 years she lived and worked abroad: in Italy, where she painted in Rome and Naples, then in Austria, and then in Russia from 1795 to 1801, where she was made a member of the Academy of Fine Arts in Saint Petersburg. In all, she was elected to art academies in ten cities.

She returned to France in 1802, spent time in London, and was back in Paris in 1805. She travelled in Switzerland in 1807 and 1808, and in 1809 settled in Louveciennes, near Paris, where she spent most of each year. In her eighties she published her memoirs, Souvenirs, with the help of her nieces. She is buried in Louveciennes. Her paintings are held by the Louvre in Paris, the Hermitage in Saint Petersburg, the National Gallery in London and the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, among many others.

STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Vigée Le Brun was above all a portrait painter, with around 660 portraits to her name, alongside landscapes. Her colours and subjects belong to the Rococo, but her style leans towards the new Neoclassicism. She disliked the stiff high fashions of her day and often asked sitters to let her change their clothes, draping them in shawls and long scarves. Before 1789 she favoured simple, floating fabrics and natural, unpowdered hair. Mothers and children were a favourite theme, and she sometimes posed her sitters as figures from mythology.

The collection reflects that range. There are several portraits of Marie Antoinette, self-portraits, and pictures of her daughter Julie at different ages. Russian and Polish sitters appear alongside French court figures such as Madame du Barry and the finance minister Calonne. Young women are shown as Flora, Atalanta and a bacchante, and there are a few drawings and a mountain landscape.

DECORATING WITH VIGÉE LE BRUN PRINTS
========================================
Vigée Le Brun's portraits of women in soft whites, blues and rose suit a bedroom, a dressing room or a quiet living room. Comtesse de la Châtre, seated in a white dress and wide dark hat, and Madame Elisabeth, with flowers in her straw hat, make a graceful pair above a dressing table or either side of a mirror.

For a hallway, library or dining room, the grander portraits of Marie Antoinette and Calonne bring the feel of a period interior. The oval Bacchante, with its wreath of leaves and blue drapery, adds warmth to a bedroom, and Paysage montagneux, a hazy mountain view, is a calm choice for a study or a gallery wall alongside the portraits.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Elisabeth Louise Vigée Le Brun part of?
A: Her work belongs to the late Rococo, with elements of Neoclassicism. Her colours and subjects are Rococo, while her simpler, classical draperies point towards Neoclassicism.

Q: What is Elisabeth Louise Vigée Le Brun known for?
A: She is best known as the portrait painter of Marie Antoinette, whom she painted, with her family, more than 30 times. She also painted aristocrats, writers and actors across Europe, and many self-portraits, some with her daughter Julie.

Q: Where can I see Vigée Le Brun's original paintings?
A: Her paintings are held by the Louvre in Paris, the Hermitage in Saint Petersburg, the National Gallery in London and the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, among many other museums in Europe and the United States. A major retrospective of her work was shown in Paris, New York and Ottawa in 2015 and 2016.

Q: How can I recognise a Vigée Le Brun portrait?
A: Look for loose, flowing fabrics, shawls and long scarves in place of stiff court dress, and natural, unpowdered hair. Some of her sitters smile openly, which critics found shocking when she showed herself smiling with her teeth visible in 1787.

Q: Was Vigée Le Brun related to the painter Charles Le Brun?
A: Not by birth. She took the name Le Brun when she married the art dealer Jean-Baptiste-Pierre Le Brun, whose great-great-uncle was Charles Le Brun, the first director of the French Academy under Louis XIV.
