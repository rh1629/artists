ABOUT BENJAMIN WEST
========================================
Benjamin West was born on 10 October 1738 in Springfield, Pennsylvania, the tenth child of an innkeeper. He was largely self-taught. Late in life he told his biographer John Galt that, as a boy, Native Americans had shown him how to make paint from river clay and bear grease. He had some training from the artisan painter William Williams, and in the 1750s he worked in Pennsylvania, mostly as a portrait painter. From the London-trained painter John Wollaston he learned how to paint the shimmer of silk and satin.

In 1760 wealthy Philadelphia patrons paid for him to travel to Italy, where he copied the works of Titian and Raphael and met neoclassical artists including Anton Raphael Mengs, Gavin Hamilton and Angelica Kauffmann. He arrived in England in August 1763, meaning only to visit on his way home. He never returned to America. In 1765 he married Elizabeth Shewell, whom he had known in Philadelphia, and classical subjects such as Pylades and Orestes soon made his name. In England he was called the "American Raphael".

West won the support of King George III and played a large part in founding the Royal Academy in 1768. The Death of General Wolfe, painted in 1770 and shown at the Academy in 1771, became one of the most reproduced images of its time. In 1772 the king made him historical painter to the court, and he painted scenes from the life of Edward III for Windsor Castle and a long series on revealed religion for a planned royal chapel. He became Surveyor of the King's Pictures in 1791, and in 1792 he succeeded Joshua Reynolds as president of the Royal Academy. He resigned in 1805 but was re-elected the next year and served until his death. Many American painters trained with him in London, among them Gilbert Stuart, John Trumbull, Charles Willson Peale and Thomas Sully.

After losing royal patronage in the early 1800s, West turned to very large religious paintings, including Christ Healing the Sick and Death on the Pale Horse, exhibited in 1817. He died at his house in Newman Street, London, on 11 March 1820 and was buried in St Paul's Cathedral. His work is held by museums in Britain and North America, including the National Gallery of Canada, the Philadelphia Museum of Art, the National Gallery of Art in Washington and the Scottish National Gallery.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
West is best known for large history paintings in the neoclassical manner: crowded, stage-like scenes in which expressive figures, colour and composition draw the viewer into a moment of drama. He called this "epic representation". He painted ancient heroes, Bible stories and recent events alike, from the deaths of Wolfe and Nelson to Franklin's kite experiment. His later work, especially Death on the Pale Horse, moved towards the wilder, more emotional mood of the Sublime and early Romanticism.

The collection covers this range and more. There are classical stories such as Hector Taking Leave of Andromache and Venus and Adonis, Bible scenes from Belshazzar's feast to the Last Supper, and Shakespeare subjects from King Lear, Hamlet and Romeo and Juliet. There are portraits of George III and Queen Charlotte, and tender pictures of West's wife Elizabeth and their son Raphael. Quieter still are landscape drawings of Windsor Great Park, Henstead in Suffolk, Paddington Canal and Ramsgate, and a long run of sketches, figure studies and drawings of vases.

DECORATING WITH WEST PRINTS
========================================
West's big history paintings are made for walls with presence: a hallway, a staircase or above a fireplace in a traditional living room. Scenes such as The Battle of La Hogue or Benjamin Franklin Drawing Electricity from the Sky have strong reds, dark skies and plenty of movement, so they suit a study or library where you want a talking point.

For a softer look, his landscape drawings are calm and light in tone. Windsor Great Park from the Castle Terrace and The Bathing Place at Ramsgate work well in a bedroom, a guest room or a small gallery wall. His domestic scenes, such as Woman at a Square Piano, and his many vase studies suit a music room, a dining room or a quiet corner, especially in pairs or a set of three.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Benjamin West part of?
A: West was a leading neoclassical history painter. His later works, such as Death on the Pale Horse, also show the influence of the Sublime and early Romanticism.

Q: What is Benjamin West known for?
A: He is best known for The Death of General Wolfe (1770), one of the most reproduced images of its time. He was also history painter to George III, the second president of the Royal Academy, and teacher to a generation of American painters in London.

Q: Where can I see Benjamin West's original works?
A: The Death of General Wolfe is in the National Gallery of Canada in Ottawa, Benjamin Franklin Drawing Electricity from the Sky is in the Philadelphia Museum of Art, and the largest Death on the Pale Horse is at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts. In Britain, the Scottish National Gallery holds The Death of the Stag and the Walker Art Gallery in Liverpool holds The Death of Nelson.

Q: How can I recognise Benjamin West's style?
A: Look for large, crowded scenes from history, myth or the Bible, with figures in strong, expressive poses arranged like actors on a stage. In his early portraits he often gave sitters large, almond-shaped eyes, a habit he picked up from John Wollaston.

Q: Is Benjamin West the painter the same as Benjamin West the astronomer?
A: No. Benjamin West the astronomer (1730–1813) was an American mathematician and almanac writer from Rhode Island. The painter was born in Pennsylvania in 1738 and spent his career in London.
