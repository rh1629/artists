ABOUT GIOVANNI BATTISTA TIEPOLO
========================================
Giovanni Battista Tiepolo was born in Venice in 1696 and baptised there that April. From about 1710 he trained with the painter Gregorio Lazzarini, and by 1717 he was a member of the Venetian painters' guild. He learned from Giovanni Battista Piazzetta and Sebastiano Ricci, but his greatest debt was to the earlier Venetian painter Paolo Veronese.

In 1719 he married Cecilia Guardi, sister of the painters Francesco and Giovanni Antonio Guardi. Two of their sons, Giovanni Domenico and Lorenzo, became his assistants. From the 1720s his commissions took him beyond Venice, to the archbishop's palace in Udine, to Milan and to Bergamo. At home he painted the ceiling of the Gesuati church, canvases for the Scuola Grande dei Carmini, and the Cleopatra frescoes of the Palazzo Labia.

In 1750 he went to Würzburg with his sons to decorate the residence of the Prince-Bishop. There he painted the Kaisersaal and the great staircase ceiling, finished in 1753, before returning to Venice. In 1755 he was elected director of the Venice Academy, and in 1757 he decorated the Villa Valmarana in Vicenza.

King Charles III of Spain commissioned him in 1761, and in 1762 he travelled to Madrid with his sons to paint ceilings in the Royal Palace, including the throne room. In Spain he faced opposition from the Neoclassical painter Anton Raphael Mengs, and his canvases for the church of San Pascual at Aranjuez were later replaced. He died in Madrid in 1770. Many of his ceilings remain where he painted them, and museums including the Prado, the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the National Gallery in London and the National Gallery of Art in Washington hold his paintings.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Tiepolo is the leading painter of the Italian Rococo. His style grew out of the grand manner of the High Renaissance and the pageantry of Veronese, but his frescoes are light in colour and airy in feel. Pale skies open overhead, and gods, saints and allegorical figures drift on banks of cloud. He was also an etcher: his two series, the Capricci and the Scherzi di Fantasia, were made in the 1740s and 1750s.

The collection reflects that range. There are studies for ceilings, with women, putti and river gods on clouds, often seen from below; scenes from history and legend such as The Banquet of Cleopatra, Queen Zenobia and Rinaldo and Armida; and religious works including the Adoration of the Magi, the Holy Family and the Flight into Egypt. Another group shows battles, sieges, coronations and processions, and there are more than a dozen caricatures of Venetians in cloaks and tricorne hats, seen from behind or in profile.

DECORATING WITH TIEPOLO PRINTS
========================================
Tiepolo's figures on clouds, such as Two Women and a Winged Boy on Clouds or A Woman Seated on a Cloud, Seen from Below, bring a sense of open sky to a room. Hang them high, over a bed, a sofa or a stairway, where they echo his painted ceilings. Grand history scenes like The Banquet of Cleopatra suit a dining room, where a painted feast sits naturally above the table.

The caricatures of Venetians in cloaks and tricornes have a dry humour that works in a hallway, study or cloakroom, especially hung as a set; try Caricature of a Man Holding a Tricorne, Walking to the Left. The Scherzi di Fantasia etchings and the head studies pair well in a library or reading corner, and the religious works, from the Holy Family to the Adoration of the Magi, suit a quiet bedroom or landing.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Giovanni Battista Tiepolo part of?
A: Tiepolo was the leading painter of the Italian Rococo, working in the Venetian tradition of Veronese. In Spain, late in life, his work was challenged by the Neoclassical painter Anton Raphael Mengs.

Q: What is Giovanni Battista Tiepolo known for?
A: He is best known for huge ceiling frescoes full of light, sky and floating figures, above all at the Würzburg Residence in Germany and the Royal Palace in Madrid. He also painted on canvas, drew, and made two series of etchings, the Capricci and the Scherzi di Fantasia.

Q: Where can I see Giovanni Battista Tiepolo's original works?
A: Many of his ceilings and wall paintings are still in place: in Venice at the Palazzo Labia, the Gesuati church and the Scuola Grande dei Carmini, in Udine, at the Würzburg Residence and at the Royal Palace in Madrid. Museums with his paintings include the Prado, the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the National Gallery in London and the National Gallery of Art in Washington.

Q: What makes Giovanni Battista Tiepolo's style recognisable?
A: Look up: pale skies, banks of cloud and figures seen from below, as if floating overhead. His colours are light and his scenes airy, even when the subject is a grand historical or religious story.

Q: Is Giovanni Battista Tiepolo the same artist as Giovanni Domenico Tiepolo?
A: No. Giovanni Domenico, often called Giandomenico, was his son, born in 1727, who worked as his assistant in Würzburg and Madrid and later became known in his own right. Their works are sometimes confused, and some paintings have been attributed to each of them at different times.
