ABOUT HENRYK SIEMIRADZKI
========================================
Henryk Siemiradzki was born on 24 October 1843 in Novo-Belgorod, now Pechenihy, near Kharkiv in present-day Ukraine. His father, Hipolit, was an officer in the Russian army, but the children were brought up in the Polish and Catholic tradition. He first learned to paint at school in Kharkiv, under Dmytro Bezperchy, a former student of Karl Briullov. At his father's wish he then studied natural sciences at Kharkiv University, writing his dissertation on the instincts of insects.

He gave up science for art and studied painting at the Imperial Academy of Arts in St Petersburg from 1864, graduating with a gold medal. A scholarship then took him to Munich, where he studied under Karl von Piloty and met Polish painters including Józef Brandt and Stanisław Witkiewicz. In 1872 he travelled through Italy and settled in Rome, which stayed his home for the rest of his life; he later built a studio there on Via Gaeta. In 1873 he married his cousin Maria Prószyńska, and they had four children. The same year the Imperial Academy made him an Academician for his painting Christ and the Sinner.

Success followed quickly. From 1876 to 1879 he worked on frescoes for the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour in Moscow, and in 1878 he received the French Legion of Honour and a gold medal at the Paris World's Fair. In 1879 he gave his huge painting Nero's Torches to the city of Kraków, a gift that led to the founding of the National Museum there. In 1884 he bought a manor house at Strzałków, near Radomsko, where the family spent their summers. He painted the stage curtains for the City Theatre in Kraków, now the Juliusz Słowacki Theatre, in 1894, and for the theatre in Lviv around 1900.

In 1901 he fell ill and lost his speech. He died at Strzałków on 23 August 1902 and was buried in Warsaw's Powązki Cemetery; in 1903 his remains were moved to the crypt of honoured Poles at Skałka in Kraków. His large canvases hang in the national museums of Poland, Ukraine and Russia, including the National Museum in Kraków, where Nero's Torches is shown in the Siemiradzki Room of the Sukiennice, the National Museum in Warsaw, the State Russian Museum in St Petersburg, the Tretyakov Gallery in Moscow and the Lviv National Art Gallery. The Detroit Institute of Arts holds his 1881 portrait of Princess Marie Lubomirska.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Siemiradzki was an academic painter who took his subjects from the ancient world. His compositions are epic and theatrical but calm and harmonious, with carefully chosen colour and settings full of figures, ornaments, trees, marble and carved reliefs. His history paintings set the luxury and cruelty of late imperial Rome against the faith of the first Christians. Alongside them he painted idylls: mostly female figures in classical dress in sun-drenched Mediterranean landscapes, which sold well to private collectors. He also painted religious commissions, allegories, portraits and landscapes.

The collection reflects all of this. Idylls of girls at wells, fountains and springs sit beside Roman scenes such as Orgy On Capri In The Time Of Tiberius (1881), Nero's Torches with its studies, and several versions of the Judgement of Paris (1892). There are portraits, from Princess Marie Lubomirska to the painter Aleksander Stankiewicz, and quieter landscapes, including a View of the Świsłocz River (1873), the river where his wife's family lived. Many of the prints are drawings and sketches, a number with Polish titles, made for the Kraków and Lviv theatre curtains and for ceiling paintings for a palace in St Petersburg.

DECORATING WITH SIEMIRADZKI PRINTS
========================================
Siemiradzki's idylls, with their warm light, pale stone and greenery, suit rooms where you want calm and a little sunshine: a living room, a garden room or a bedroom. Water scenes such as Girl at the Well or Faun's pond work well on their own above a sofa or bed, or as a pair in a hallway.

His large history scenes, such as Nero's Torches or The Judgement of Paris, have the drama for a dining room or a long wall that can take a wide print. Landscapes such as Italian landscape with a donkey are a gentle fit for a study or kitchen, and his sketches and studies look good grouped together in a study, library or stairway.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Henryk Siemiradzki part of?
A: Siemiradzki belonged to academic art, also called academism. He trained at the Imperial Academy of Arts in St Petersburg and in Munich under Karl von Piloty.

Q: What is Henryk Siemiradzki known for?
A: He is best known for large history paintings of the ancient Greco-Roman world and the New Testament, above all Nero's Torches. He also painted the stage curtains for theatres in Kraków and Lviv.

Q: Where can I see Henryk Siemiradzki's original works?
A: Nero's Torches hangs in the Siemiradzki Room of the Sukiennice, part of the National Museum in Kraków. Other major works are in the National Museum in Warsaw, the State Russian Museum in St Petersburg, the Tretyakov Gallery in Moscow and the Lviv National Art Gallery, and the Detroit Institute of Arts holds his portrait of Princess Marie Lubomirska.

Q: What makes Henryk Siemiradzki's style recognizable?
A: Look for ancient settings full of marble, ornaments and trees, with figures in classical dress, often in bright Mediterranean sunlight. Even his dramatic scenes have a calm, balanced composition.

Q: Was Henryk Siemiradzki Polish or Russian?
A: He was a Polish painter, born in the Russian Empire near Kharkiv and brought up in the Polish tradition. He trained in St Petersburg and took on commissions in Moscow, so his name also appears in the Russian form, Semiradsky.
