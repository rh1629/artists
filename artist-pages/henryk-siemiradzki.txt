========================================
ABOUT HENRYK SIEMIRADZKI
========================================
Henryk Siemiradzki was born in 1843 in Novo-Belgorod, near Kharkiv in today's Ukraine, where his father, a Polish nobleman and an officer in the Imperial Russian Army, was stationed with his regiment. He first learned to paint at school in Kharkiv. To please his father, he then studied natural sciences at Kharkiv University and kept painting on the side.

He gave up science to study painting at the Imperial Academy of Arts in Saint Petersburg, where he won a gold medal. A grant from the Academy took him to Munich to study under Karl von Piloty, and there he met fellow Polish painters, among them Józef Brandt and Stanisław Witkiewicz. In 1872 he moved to Rome. He later built a studio there on Via Gaeta, and Rome remained his base for the rest of his career.

Success came quickly. In 1873 the Academy made him an Academician for his painting Christ and a Sinner, and in 1878 he received the French Legion of Honour and a gold medal at the Paris World's Fair. In 1879 he gave his enormous painting Nero's Torches to the newly formed National Museum in Kraków, a gift that started its national collection. He also painted frescoes for the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour in Moscow and the stage curtains of the city theatres in Kraków (1894) and Lviv (1900).

From 1884 he spent his summers with his wife Maria and their four children at a manor house he bought in Strzałków, in central Poland. He fell ill in 1901 and lost his speech, and he died at Strzałków in 1902. He was buried in Warsaw, and in 1903 his remains were moved to the national pantheon at Skałka in Kraków. His large canvases are held by national museums in Poland, Ukraine and Russia, including the National Museum in Kraków, the Lviv National Art Gallery and the Tretyakov Gallery in Moscow.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Siemiradzki painted in the academic style, and most of his subjects come from the ancient Greco-Roman world and the New Testament. His compositions are large, theatrical and carefully composed, yet calm and harmonious in mood. They are filled with figures in classical dress, ornaments, trees, marble and carved reliefs. He could work on a huge scale, as in Nero's Torches, which is more than seven metres wide, but he also painted smaller idylls such as Roman Idyll (Fishing) and Faun's Pond.

The collection shows both sides of his work. Alongside history and myth scenes such as Nero's Torches, Orgy on Capri in the Time of Tiberius and the Judgement of Paris, there are many Roman idylls of women by fountains and springs, as well as Italian landscapes, portraits and studies of heads. A large part of the collection is working drawings: studies for Phryne and Christian Dirce, sketches for the Kraków and Lviv theatre curtains, and designs for painted ceilings for a palace in Saint Petersburg.

DECORATING WITH SIEMIRADZKI PRINTS
========================================
Siemiradzki's idylls, with figures resting by fountains, springs and still water, suit calm rooms: a bedroom, a garden room or a bathroom. Roman idyll – Fishing and At the Fountain work well as a pair. For a gentler, rural note in a kitchen or hallway, try one of his landscapes, such as Italian landscape with a donkey or House with hollyhocks in the garden.

The large history and myth scenes, such as Nero's Torches or the Judgement of Paris, have the presence to anchor a dining room, a library or a big living-room wall. His drawings and ceiling designs, with their loose lines and floating figures, make a good group in a study or along a staircase.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Henryk Siemiradzki part of?
A: Siemiradzki was an academic painter. He trained at the Imperial Academy of Arts in Saint Petersburg and is best remembered for large history paintings in the academic style.

Q: What is Henryk Siemiradzki known for?
A: He is known for large paintings of the ancient Greco-Roman world and the New Testament, above all Nero's Torches (1876), which he gave to Kraków in 1879. Phryne at the Festival of Poseidon in Eleusis (1889) and his painted theatre curtains for Kraków and Lviv are also well known.

Q: Where can I see Henryk Siemiradzki's original works?
A: Nero's Torches hangs in the Siemiradzki Room of the Sukiennice Museum, part of the National Museum in Kraków, on the city's Main Square. The National Museum in Warsaw holds Christian Dirce (1897), and other major works are in the State Russian Museum in Saint Petersburg, the Tretyakov Gallery in Moscow and the Lviv National Art Gallery.

Q: How can I recognise Siemiradzki's style?
A: Look for scenes from ancient Rome and Greece with many figures in classical dress, surrounded by marble, carved reliefs, ornaments and trees. The mood is usually calm and harmonious, even when the subject is dramatic.

Q: Was Henryk Siemiradzki Polish or Russian?
A: He was a Polish painter, born to a Polish noble family in the Russian Empire, in what is now Ukraine, and raised in the Polish Catholic tradition. He trained in Saint Petersburg, which is why his name also appears as Semiradsky.
