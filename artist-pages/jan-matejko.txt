========================================
ABOUT JAN MATEJKO
========================================
Jan Matejko was born in Kraków in 1838 and died there in 1893. His father was a Czech music teacher who had settled in the city, and Jan was the ninth of eleven children. As a boy he saw the Kraków revolution of 1846 and the Austrian siege of the city in 1848, and two of his older brothers fought in both. He struggled at school but drew exceptionally well, and from 1852 to 1858 he studied at the School of Fine Arts in Kraków under Wojciech Korneli Stattler and Władysław Łuszczkiewicz. Artur Grottger was a fellow student.

In 1859 he studied at the Munich Academy on a scholarship. A short stay in Vienna ended after a quarrel with his professor, and he returned to Kraków, setting up a studio in the family home on Floriańska Street. Success came slowly. In 1860 he published Clothes in Poland, an illustrated album of historical dress, and in 1862 he finished Stańczyk, the painting that turned him from an illustrator of history into a commentator on it. He gave most of his savings to the January Uprising of 1863 and carried arms to an insurgents' camp. In 1864 he married Teodora Giebułtowska, and they had five children.

Recognition followed quickly. Skarga's Sermon won a gold medal in Paris in 1865, Rejtan another in 1867, and the Union of Lublin (1869) brought him the Legion of Honour. From 1873 he led the Kraków School of Fine Arts. The great canvases kept coming: Astronomer Copernicus (1873), the Battle of Grunwald (1878), and The Prussian Homage (1880–82), which he gave to the Polish nation. In his last years he painted a series of twelve pictures on the History of Civilisation in Poland, drew every monarch of Poland in a set of portraits that became the standard images of them, and designed murals for St Mary's Basilica in Kraków. He died in Kraków on 1 November 1893 and was buried in Rakowicki Cemetery.

More than 80 painters studied under Matejko. His most important students, Jacek Malczewski, Józef Mehoffer, Stanisław Wyspiański and Leon Wyczółkowski, became leading Polish modernists, and the Kraków academy now carries his name. His paintings are held in the National Museums in Warsaw, Kraków, Poznań and Wrocław and at the Royal Castle in Warsaw. His house on Floriańska Street is now the Jan Matejko House, part of the National Museum in Kraków.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Matejko painted Polish history on a grand scale. He chose dramatic moments from the past and filled them with crowds, period costume, armour and banners, researched from historical sources. His work is known for precise outlines, close detail and a smooth, carefully finished surface. Critics have called it old-fashioned and theatrical, but his luminous colour and imagination are widely praised, and his pictures, reproduced thousands of times, became the standard images of Poland's past.

The collection shows both the finished pictures and the work behind them. Alongside history scenes such as Stańczyk and the Battle of Grunwald, it includes twelve scenes from the History of Civilisation in Poland, oil sketches for Skarga's Sermon, Wernyhora and the Constitution of 3 May, studies of horses and armour, and costume studies. There are cartoons for the painted decoration of St Mary's Basilica, portraits of his wife, children and father and of Kraków figures, and a few lighter pieces, from a still life with fruit to grotesque devils.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Jan Matejko part of?
A: Matejko was a nineteenth-century history painter, trained in the academic tradition in Kraków and Munich and counted among the leading figures of historicism. He was not part of Young Poland himself, but he has been called its father because so many of its artists were his students.

Q: What is Jan Matejko known for?
A: He is known for large paintings of key moments in Polish history, including Stańczyk, Rejtan, the Union of Lublin, the Battle of Grunwald and The Prussian Homage. He is often called the national painter of Poland, and his drawings of the kings and princes of Poland became the standard portraits of them.

Q: Where can I see Jan Matejko's original paintings?
A: The National Museum in Warsaw holds the Battle of Grunwald and Stańczyk, the Royal Castle in Warsaw holds Rejtan and the Constitution of 3 May, and the National Museum in Kraków holds The Prussian Homage. You can also visit the Jan Matejko House on Floriańska Street in Kraków, his former home and studio.

Q: How can I recognise a Matejko painting?
A: Look for a single dramatic moment from Polish history, packed with figures in carefully researched costume, armour and banners. Outlines are precise, the paint surface is smooth, and the staging often feels theatrical.

Q: Why were Matejko's paintings hidden during the Second World War?
A: Nazi Germany planned to destroy the Battle of Grunwald and The Prussian Homage, which it saw as an offence to the German view of history. The Polish resistance hid both paintings, and they survived.
