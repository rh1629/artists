========================================
ABOUT JULIAN FAŁAT
========================================
Julian Fałat was born on 30 July 1853 in Tuligłowy, near Lwów (now Lviv in Ukraine). He paid for his own art education. After finishing secondary school in Przemyśl, he entered the School of Fine Arts in Kraków in 1869, where he studied under Władysław Łuszczkiewicz. He could not afford oil paints, so he turned to watercolour, the medium that would make his name. He went on to study in Zurich and at the Munich Academy of Fine Arts, which he entered in 1875, taking breaks to earn money on railway construction.

After a year in Rome and a spell in Warsaw, where he drew scenes of city life for the magazine Kłosy, he travelled in Spain and then around the world, recording the journey in watercolours. In the late 1880s he moved to Berlin as a court painter to Wilhelm II, painting hunting scenes and winning medals at exhibitions in Berlin, Munich and Vienna. There he planned a huge panorama of Napoleon's army crossing the Berezina river. Fałat painted the snowy landscape and Wojciech Kossak the figures, and the panorama was shown in Berlin in 1896.

In 1895 Fałat returned to Kraków as director of the School of Fine Arts. He brought in new professors with modern ideas and set up a chair of landscape painting for Jan Stanisławski. In 1900 the school became the Academy of Fine Arts, which he led as rector until 1909. In 1897 he co-founded the Society of Polish Artists "Sztuka". He married Maria Luisa Comello Stuckenfeld in 1900, and they had three children. In 1902 he built a villa and studio at Bystra, and in 1910 he settled there for good.

In January 1919 he volunteered for the Polish army, serving with a squadron of Uhlans. From 1919 to 1922 he lived in Toruń, where his home became a meeting place for artists and he led the artists' fraternity Konfraternia Artystów. Retrospectives followed in Kraków and Warsaw in 1925. He died at Bystra on 9 July 1929, and his villa is now a museum, the Fałatówka. Two of his hunting paintings, looted from the National Museum in Warsaw in 1944, were found in New York and returned to Poland in 2011. His work is held by the National Museums in Kraków and Warsaw, the Silesian Museum in Katowice, the Belvedere in Vienna and the Alte Nationalgalerie in Berlin.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Fałat was above all a watercolourist, and he is counted among Poland's foremost landscape painters. His style sits between realism and impressionism: real places, closely observed, painted with an eye for light and season. Snow is one of his great subjects, in winter fields, frozen rivers and village roads. The other is the hunt. After 1887 hunting scenes became central to his work, with beaters, spearmen, hounds and hunting parties, and animals such as deer, roe deer and elk.

The collection follows him across his life. There are early studies such as Old man in narthex (1881), a sketch made on board ship at Colombo, Ceylon, in 1885, and Montmartre Cemetery in Paris (1893). Hunts at Nieśwież and Rytwiany sit beside winter landscapes from Osiek, Żywiec and Bystra, and the garden and autumn views around his home. Kraków's Barbican and Franciscan cloister and a group of 1920 views of Toruń and the Vistula round out the places, while portraits of his daughter Kuka, his brother and himself bring in the family.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Julian Fałat part of?
A: Fałat is described as a realist and impressionist landscape painter, and as one of Poland's leading impressionists. In 1897 he was a founding member of the Society of Polish Artists "Sztuka" in Kraków.

Q: What is Julian Fałat known for?
A: He is best known as a master of watercolour, and for his winter landscapes and hunting scenes. He also led the Kraków art school from 1895 to 1909, turning it into the Academy of Fine Arts.

Q: Where can I see Julian Fałat's original works?
A: The National Museums in Kraków and Warsaw hold his work, as do the Silesian Museum in Katowice, the District Museum in Toruń, the Belvedere in Vienna and the Alte Nationalgalerie in Berlin. His villa and studio in Bystra is now a museum, the Fałatówka.

Q: How can I recognise Julian Fałat's style?
A: Look for snow, open countryside and hunting scenes, often in watercolour. His pictures are rooted in real places, from Bystra, Kraków and Toruń to the hunting grounds of Nieśwież.

Q: Is Julian Falat the same artist as Julian Fałat?
A: Yes. Fałat is the Polish spelling of his name. The letter ł is often written as a plain l in English, so you will also see Julian Falat, including in our product titles.
