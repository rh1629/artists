ABOUT JULIAN FAŁAT
========================================
Julian Fałat was born on 30 July 1853 in Tuligłowy, near Lwów (now Lviv in Ukraine). He paid for his own education, without help from his family or grants. After secondary school in Przemyśl, he enrolled at the Kraków School of Fine Arts in 1869, where he studied under Władysław Łuszczkiewicz. He could not afford oil paints, so he turned to watercolour, the medium that would make his name.

After his studies he worked as a draughtsman on archaeological digs in Ukraine and in an architect's office in Odessa. In 1875 he went on to the Academy of Fine Arts in Munich, breaking off his studies to earn money as a railway technician. He then spent a year in Rome, where he painted Old Man Praying (1881). From 1882 to 1886 he worked in Warsaw, drawing scenes of city life for the magazine Kłosy. In the mid-1880s he travelled to Spain and then around the world, recording the journey in watercolours.

In 1886 he accepted an invitation from the future German Emperor Wilhelm II to work as a court painter in Berlin, and he stayed until 1895. He won medals at exhibitions in Berlin, Munich and Vienna. With Jan Stanisławski he painted the landscape parts of Wojciech Kossak's panorama of Napoleon's army crossing the Berezina. In 1895 he became director of the Kraków School of Fine Arts. He reformed it and brought in new teachers, including Stanisławski, who led a new landscape class, and in 1900 the school became the Academy of Fine Arts. He led it until 1909. In 1897 he was one of the founders of the Society of Polish Artists "Sztuka".

In 1900 he married Maria Luiza Comello Stuckenfeld, and they had three children. In 1902 he built a villa with a studio at Bystra, in the hills of southern Poland, and settled there for good in 1910. From 1919 to 1922 he lived in Toruń, where his house became a meeting place for artists and he co-founded and led the Artists' Confraternity. He died at Bystra on 9 July 1929. His villa is now a museum, part of the Bielsko-Biała Museum. The National Museums in Kraków and Warsaw, the Silesian Museum in Katowice, the Belvedere in Vienna and the Alte Nationalgalerie in Berlin hold his work. Two of his hunting paintings, looted from Warsaw in the Second World War, were found at New York auction houses and returned to Poland in 2011.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Fałat is counted among Poland's leading watercolourists and landscape painters. His work moved from realism towards an impressionist way of painting landscape. He painted Polish landscapes, hunting scenes, portraits and things he saw on his travels. Hunts became a major theme after 1887, with deer, roe deer and, from 1891, elk. After 1900 winter landscapes took a central place, and he painted the walls of Kraków again and again, seen from his studio window at different times of day. In his own words, Polish art "must be the quintessence of our soil, our sky, our ideals."

The collection follows these themes closely. There are hunts at Nieśwież, Rytwiany and Staszów, elk and roe deer in the forest, and sleighs crossing snowy fields in Polesia. Winter rivers, snow-laden trees and autumn views of Bystra show the landscapes of his later years. Kraków appears in the Barbican, the Franciscan cloister and the city in the morning, and a group from 1920 records Toruń and the Vistula. There are also portraits of his daughter Kuka, a woman in a kimono, and quick sketches from his travels, including a scene on a ship at Colombo in 1885.

DECORATING WITH FAŁAT PRINTS
========================================
Fałat's winter scenes are mostly white, pale blue and soft grey, so they suit light, calm rooms: a bedroom, a hallway or a living room with pale walls. Snow, with its dark stream cutting through deep drifts under a clear blue sky, makes a strong single piece above a sofa or bed. Sleigh in a snowy field – Polesia, with its haystacks and long shadows, pairs well with it. The quiet grey and white of Boats and steamship on the Vistula near Toruń suits a study or reading corner.

The hunting scenes, with their dark figures and snowy pine forests, have the feel of a country house and work well in a study, library or dining room, alone or as a set. For warmer colour, Garden in Bystra brings deep summer greens into a kitchen or garden room. The 1910 Woman in kimono, with its dark patterned robe and flash of turquoise, makes a striking portrait for a bedroom or dressing area.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Julian Fałat part of?
A: Fałat is linked with realism and with Impressionism in landscape, and he is described as one of Poland's leading impressionists. In 1897 he was a founding member of the Society of Polish Artists "Sztuka" in Kraków.

Q: What is Julian Fałat known for?
A: He is known as one of the finest Polish watercolourists. His best-known subjects are winter landscapes and hunting scenes, along with views of Kraków and portraits.

Q: Where can I see Julian Fałat's original works?
A: The National Museum in Kraków and the National Museum in Warsaw hold important works, as do the Silesian Museum in Katowice and the District Museum in Toruń. His villa at Bystra, the Fałatówka, is now a museum devoted to him, part of the Bielsko-Biała Museum.

Q: How can I recognise Julian Fałat's style?
A: Look for snow: winter fields, rivers winding through drifts, and hunting parties in snowy forests, very often painted in watercolour. His views of Kraków's walls and towers are another sign of his hand.

Q: Was Julian Fałat a court painter?
A: Yes. At the invitation of the future German Emperor Wilhelm II he worked as a court painter in Berlin from 1886 to 1895, before returning to lead the art school in Kraków.
