ABOUT AUGUST BABBERGER
========================================
August Babberger was born on 8 December 1885 in Hausen im Wiesental, in Baden in south-west Germany. He was the son of a carpenter and grew up in Basel, where his family moved in 1895. He trained as a house painter, travelled widely as a journeyman, and tried without success to enter the art academy in Munich.

In 1908, while working as a decorator in Karlsruhe, he showed his pen drawings to the painter Hans Thoma. Thoma advised him to stay and study at the Karlsruhe Academy, where he joined the etching class. The following year Thoma helped him win a scholarship to the International Art School in Florence. Babberger spent two winters there, drawing and painting mostly nudes, and began a lifelong friendship with the Swiss painter Augusto Giacometti.

From 1915 he turned to pure landscape painting in the Swiss Alps, and from 1918 he spent regular summers in an Alpine hut on the Klausen Pass in the canton of Uri. After marrying Anna Maria Tobler, a reverse glass painter and poet from Lucerne, he moved to Frankfurt. In 1919 he was a founding member of the Darmstadt Secession. In 1920 he became professor of decorative painting at the Karlsruhe Academy, and he was its director from 1923 to 1930. His work was entered in the art competition of the 1928 Olympic Games in Amsterdam.

From 1925 a loose circle of Swiss artists, writers and musicians, the Urner Kreis, gathered around him in Uri, including his students Heinrich Danioth and Erna Schillig. In July 1933 the Nazi authorities branded him a "degenerate" artist and removed him from his teaching post, and he moved to his wife's home country, Switzerland. He died in Altdorf, Uri, on 3 September 1936, from the after-effects of a throat operation, at the age of 50. His estate was taken to Altdorf, out of the Nazis' reach, but in 1937 nine of his works were seized from German public collections. Today his work is held by the Staatliche Kunsthalle Karlsruhe, the Dreiländermuseum in Lörrach and the Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art in Strasbourg.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Babberger's early etchings and figure paintings, made during his student years in Karlsruhe, belong to the world of Symbolism and Jugendstil, and show the influence of Ferdinand Hodler, Arnold Böcklin and Hans Thoma. In the Alps he found his main subject, and an ideal of people, art and nature living as one. In his major works of the 1920s and 1930s he arrived at an Expressionist style of radically simplified forms and intense, heightened colour. He also designed murals, mosaics and stained glass, and from 1918 he made woodcuts that turn people, nature and the cosmos into strict, compact, flat signs.

The collection is strongest on mountains: the Scheerhorn, the Balmalp and the Schächental, the Klausen Pass, the Gental and the Bergell valley, with peaks broken into angular blue, white and green shapes, often under a small bright sun. Alongside them are figures in nature, such as Paare im Walde II, and black-and-white sheets like Freunde in der Natur and Frau im Frühling. There are songbook designs for Schubert, Schumann, Beethoven and Hugo Wolf, dense flower pieces like Blumen am Berg and Gartenblumen I, delicate plant studies, a view of Florence, and portraits that include several self-portraits.

DECORATING WITH BABBERGER PRINTS
========================================
Babberger's Alpine landscapes, with their deep blues, snowy whites and sharp greens, bring energy and fresh mountain air to a living room or hallway. Hochgebirgslandschaft mit Scheerhorn or Kuhherde im Hochgebirge make a strong single piece above a sofa or fireplace, while the lighter, airier Landschaft am Klausenpass (Nachmittag) suits a study or bedroom.

For a kitchen, dining room or garden room, flower pieces such as Blumen am Berg and Gartenblumen I add dense, cheerful colour. The black-and-white sheets, from Freunde in der Natur to the composer songbooks such as Franz Schubert Lieder-Buch, look sharp in a monochrome scheme or a music room, and work well hung together as a set.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was August Babberger part of?
A: Babberger was an Expressionist and is counted among the leading figures of early modern art in south-west Germany. His early work grew out of Symbolism and Jugendstil, and in 1919 he was a founding member of the Darmstadt Secession.

Q: What is August Babberger known for?
A: He is best known for his Expressionist paintings of the Swiss Alps, many made during summers on the Klausen Pass in Uri. He also made woodcuts, designed murals, mosaics and stained glass, and directed the Karlsruhe Academy from 1923 to 1930.

Q: Why was August Babberger called a "degenerate" artist?
A: After the Nazis came to power, he was branded a "degenerate" artist in July 1933 and lost his post at the Karlsruhe Academy. In 1937 nine of his works were seized from German public collections in the "Degenerate Art" campaign.

Q: Where can I see August Babberger's original works?
A: The Staatliche Kunsthalle Karlsruhe holds several of his paintings, including Hochgebirgslandschaft mit Scheerhorn and Balmalp. You can also see the choir mosaic made from his design in the Stiftskirche in Neustadt an der Weinstraße, and his painted interior of the Reformed church in Wolhusen, in the canton of Lucerne.

Q: How can I recognise August Babberger's style?
A: Look for mountains broken into bold, angular shapes in strong blues, greens and whites, often with a small bright sun. His figures and woodcuts use the same simplified, flat forms.
