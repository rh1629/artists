ABOUT JEAN-HONORÉ FRAGONARD
========================================
Jean-Honoré Fragonard was born in Grasse, in Provence, on 5 April 1732, the son of a glover. His family moved to Paris when he was six. He trained with Jean-Siméon Chardin and then with François Boucher, and in 1752 he won the Prix de Rome with Jeroboam Sacrificing to the Idols. He went on to study under Charles-André van Loo at the École royale des élèves protégés.

From 1756 to 1761 he was at the French Academy in Rome. He travelled in Italy with his friend the painter Hubert Robert and with the Abbé de Saint-Non, who became his patron, and he drew gardens, villas and the old masters. He admired Tiepolo and the Dutch and Flemish painters. Back in Paris, his large history painting Coresus Sacrificing Himself to Save Callirhoe won him entry to the Royal Academy in 1765. The king bought it, and the critic Denis Diderot praised it.

Rather than keep to grand history painting, Fragonard turned to smaller, intimate scenes for private collectors, including Madame du Barry and the dancer Madeleine Guimard. In 1769 he married Marie-Anne Gérard, a miniature painter also from Grasse. Their daughter Rosalie was a favourite model, and their son Alexandre-Évariste became a painter too. His sister-in-law Marguerite Gérard became his pupil and assistant in 1778. In 1773 and 1774 he made a second journey to Italy and central Europe.

The French Revolution cost him his patrons. He spent 1790 and 1791 in Grasse with his cousin, and was later appointed one of the curators of the new Louvre museum, with the support of Jacques-Louis David. He died in Paris on 22 August 1806, largely forgotten. His reputation recovered in the 19th century, and the Impressionists, including Renoir, admired his brushwork and colour. His paintings are now in the Louvre, the Wallace Collection in London, the Frick Collection and the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, and the National Gallery of Art in Washington.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Fragonard is one of the leading painters of the French Rococo. His brushwork is fast and confident, with expressive, graceful gestures and drapery full of energy. He is best known for playful scenes of love and flirtation, set in gardens under towering trees or in candlelit bedrooms, but he also painted landscapes inspired by Dutch artists, religious and mythological pictures, and warm scenes of family life. His "fantasy figures", painted very quickly, show sitters in theatrical costume.

The collection reflects that range. Alongside The Swing, Young Girl Reading and The Bolt, there are Italian garden views such as the Villa d'Este at Tivoli and the gardens of the Villa Medici, children's games and family scenes, and portraits of young women and boys. Many of the prints are drawings: a long series illustrating Ariosto's poem Orlando Furioso, scenes from Don Quixote, and a set of Bacchanal etchings from 1763 with dancing satyrs and nymphs.

DECORATING WITH FRAGONARD PRINTS
========================================
Fragonard's garden scenes, with their soft greens, blues and rose pinks, suit a bedroom, dressing room or sitting room where you want something light and romantic. The Swing makes a natural centrepiece, and The Musical Contest or Blind-Man's Buff pair well with it. His Italian views, such as The Grand Staircase of the Villa d'Este at Tivoli, have a calmer, greener palette that works in a hallway, study or dining room.

For a reading corner or library, Young Girl Reading is an obvious choice. His children and family scenes, such as A Boy as Pierrot or The Visit to the Nursery, bring warmth to a nursery, family room or landing. The pen and wash drawings from Orlando Furioso and Don Quixote look good grouped together on a staircase wall or in a study.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Jean-Honoré Fragonard part of?
A: Fragonard was one of the leading painters of the French Rococo. He is often seen as the last great painter of that era, whose style went out of fashion with the Neoclassicism of Jacques-Louis David and the French Revolution.

Q: What is Jean-Honoré Fragonard known for?
A: He is best known for The Swing, Young Girl Reading and The Bolt, and for playful, sensual scenes of love set in gardens and bedrooms. He also painted landscapes, family scenes and quickly painted costume portraits known as fantasy figures.

Q: Where can I see Jean-Honoré Fragonard's original works?
A: Major collections include the Louvre in Paris, the Wallace Collection in London, the Frick Collection and the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, and the National Gallery of Art in Washington. His home town of Grasse has the Villa-Musée Jean-Honoré Fragonard.

Q: How can I recognise Fragonard's style?
A: Look for quick, visible brushstrokes, swirling drapery and lively, graceful gestures. His scenes are often set under huge, feathery trees, in soft pinks, greens and golds, with a sense of fleeting pleasure.

Q: Is Jean-Honoré Fragonard related to Honoré Fragonard, the anatomist?
A: Yes. Honoré Fragonard, famous for his anatomical models, was his cousin. Those models are kept in the Fragonard Museum at the veterinary school in Maisons-Alfort, near Paris.
