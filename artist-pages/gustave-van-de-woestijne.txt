ABOUT GUSTAVE VAN DE WOESTIJNE
========================================
Gustave Van de Woestijne was born in Ghent on 2 August 1881 and died on 21 April 1947. He was the younger brother of the Flemish poet Karel van de Woestijne. He studied at the Ghent Academy from 1895 to 1900, but illness kept him from finishing his final year.

Around 1900 he moved with Karel to Sint-Martens-Latem, a village on the river Lys near Ghent where poets, painters and sculptors had begun to gather. He became part of what is now called the first Latem group, and there he met Valerius De Saedeleer, Albijn Van den Abeele and many other artists. He painted village life, biblical scenes and portraits of local people. In 1905 he briefly entered a Benedictine monastery but soon decided against religious life, and he left Latem in 1909.

When the First World War began in 1914, he fled Belgium with the De Saedeleer family, travelling by way of Ostend and London to Wales, where he lived for a time in the seaside town of Aberystwyth. During the war years he regularly visited the circle of De Saedeleer and the sculptor George Minne. He returned to Belgium in the summer of 1919 and settled with his family in the Rozenhuis, a house in Waregem. The early 1920s were among his most productive years and include The Blind Violinist (1920), now at La Boverie in Liège. He was made a Knight of the Order of Leopold in 1919.

From the mid-1920s he taught at the academy in Mechelen, which he later directed, and he also taught painting at La Cambre in Brussels. He is buried at the Campo Santo in Ghent. The Museum of Fine Arts in Ghent held a memorial exhibition in 1949 and showed its whole holding of his work in 2020. His paintings are also in the Royal Museum of Fine Arts Antwerp, the Groeningemuseum in Bruges and La Boverie in Liège.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Van de Woestijne's early work grew out of Symbolism and a deep religious feeling. He admired the technique and spirituality of the Flemish Primitives and the Italian Quattrocento, and it shows in his clear outlines, refined detail and still, composed poses. Latem villagers often appear in his religious scenes, and his peasant pictures of 1910 to 1912 look back to the Primitives and to Bruegel. His flat, simplified backgrounds already point towards modern art, and after Latem he found his own balance between Expressionism and Cubism, while keeping a meditative, symbolic mood.

The collection reflects that range. There are sober portraits of farmers and peasant women, a blind man in a dark cloak, and a haloed Christ showing his wounds. Works such as Fugue and Azure set solemn faces against angular houses and trees, while Gaston and his Sister and Two Springs bring in warmer colour. Pencil portraits of his brother Karel (1928) and of Hippolyte Fierens-Gevaert (1912), and a soft-toned Still Life with Grapes (1937), show his quieter side.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Gustave Van de Woestijne part of?
A: He belonged to the first Latem group, artists who worked in the village of Sint-Martens-Latem near Ghent. His early work is linked with Symbolism, and he is also described as a Belgian expressionist painter.

Q: What is Gustave Van de Woestijne known for?
A: He is known for portraits of village people, peasant scenes and religious paintings with clear outlines and still poses, inspired by the Flemish Primitives.

Q: Where can I see Gustave Van de Woestijne's original works?
A: The Museum of Fine Arts (MSK) in Ghent holds a large group of his works, and the Royal Museum of Fine Arts Antwerp (KMSKA) also has important paintings. His Last Supper (1927) is at the Groeningemuseum in Bruges, and The Blind Violinist (1920) is at La Boverie in Liège.

Q: Is Gustave Van de Woestijne the same person as Karel van de Woestijne?
A: No. Karel van de Woestijne was his older brother, a Flemish poet, while Gustave was the painter. The two moved to Sint-Martens-Latem together around 1900.

Q: Is it Van de Woestyne or Van de Woestijne?
A: Both spellings are used for the same artist. The Museum of Fine Arts in Ghent writes Van de Woestyne, while many other sources write Van de Woestijne.
