========================================
ABOUT FRANCES HODGKINS
========================================
Frances Hodgkins was born in Dunedin, New Zealand, on 28 April 1869. Her father, William Mathew Hodgkins, was a lawyer and a keen amateur watercolourist. She took classes from the visiting Italian painter G. P. Nerli in 1893 and studied at the Dunedin School of Art in 1895–96.

In 1901 she sailed for Europe, where she lived for the rest of her life apart from two return visits. She took classes in London and joined Norman Garstin's summer sketching classes in France, then travelled and painted in France, the Netherlands and Morocco. In 1910 she became the first woman to teach at the Académie Colarossi in Paris, and she went on to run her own watercolour school there.

During the First World War she lived in St Ives, Cornwall, where she met the painter Cedric Morris, who became a lifelong friend. In the mid-1920s she worked as a fabric designer for the Calico Printers' Association in Manchester. Her place in British modernism was confirmed when she joined the Seven and Five Society, exhibiting with them from 1929 to 1934, and in 1940 she was selected for the British pavilion at the Venice Biennale, although the war stopped the work reaching Venice.

From 1934 she worked in Dorset, settling at Corfe Castle in 1940. She died near Dorchester on 13 May 1947. Her work is held by Auckland Art Gallery Toi o Tāmaki, Te Papa, Dunedin Public Art Gallery, the Hocken Collections, Christchurch Art Gallery, Tate and the National Galleries of Scotland.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Hodgkins began as a watercolourist, painting people and places on her travels. From the late 1920s her style became fully modern: forms were simplified, and colour and the balance between colours did most of the work. Her best-known theme, drawn from Picasso and Matisse, sets a still life in the foreground against a landscape behind it, so that a table of objects sits in front of a harbour, a field or a hillside.

Late in life she turned to oil and to gouache, a watercolour thickened with white, which gave her forms more weight. Her later subjects come from the places she worked: the view from her rooms in Cornwall, farms and valleys in Wales, and the village of Corfe Castle in Dorset. She also painted portraits, and one of her best-loved works is a self-portrait made entirely of her belongings.

========================================
FAMOUS WORKS
========================================
- Double Portrait (c. 1922–25), Hocken Collections, University of Otago, Dunedin. A figure painting from the years before her move to modernism.
- Wings over Water (c. 1931–32), Tate, London. Painted from her rooms at Bodinnick in Cornwall, it joins a still life to the view beyond and marks a key step towards her mature style.
- Pleasure Garden (1932), Christchurch Art Gallery Te Puna o Waiwhetū. Offered to Christchurch after her death, it set off a public row over modern art that lasted until the city accepted it in 1951.
- Self Portrait: Still Life (c. 1935), Auckland Art Gallery Toi o Tāmaki. A self-portrait without a face: her beret, scarf, shoes and bag stand in for the painter.
- Green Valley, Carmarthenshire (1942), Dunedin Public Art Gallery. A wartime gouache of the Welsh countryside.
- Corfe Castle (1943), Dunedin Public Art Gallery. An oil of the Dorset village where she spent her last years.

========================================
DECORATING WITH HODGKINS PRINTS
========================================
Hodgkins's still lifes set in landscapes are natural centrepieces for a living room or dining room. They bring together the comfort of everyday objects and the openness of a view, and their soft, balanced colour sits easily with both modern and traditional furniture.

Her Welsh and Dorset scenes, with their greens and blues, suit calm spaces such as a bedroom, a study or a hallway. For a dressing area or a reading corner, her still lifes of personal belongings make a quiet, personal choice, and a pair of prints from her late period works well hung side by side.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Frances Hodgkins part of?
A: Hodgkins is counted among the leading modernist painters in Britain. She was a member of the Seven and Five Society, a London group of modern artists, from 1929 to 1934.

Q: What is Frances Hodgkins known for?
A: She is best known for her late paintings that set a still life against a landscape, painted with simplified forms and rich colour. She was also the first woman to teach at the Académie Colarossi in Paris, in 1910.

Q: Where can I see Frances Hodgkins's original works?
A: In New Zealand, her work is held by Auckland Art Gallery Toi o Tāmaki, Te Papa, Dunedin Public Art Gallery, the Hocken Collections and Christchurch Art Gallery. In Britain, Tate and the National Galleries of Scotland hold her paintings.

Q: What makes Frances Hodgkins's style recognisable?
A: Look for everyday objects placed in front of an open landscape. Her forms are simplified and her colour does much of the work, especially in her late oils and gouaches.

Q: Why was Frances Hodgkins's Pleasure Garden controversial?
A: When local subscribers offered it to the city's art gallery after her death, Christchurch City Council turned it down as too avant-garde. After a long public campaign, the city accepted it in 1951, and it is now in Christchurch Art Gallery.
