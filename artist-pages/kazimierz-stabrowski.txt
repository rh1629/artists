ABOUT KAZIMIERZ STABROWSKI
========================================
Kazimierz Stabrowski was born in 1869 in the Grodno region, in what is now Belarus, into a Polish landed gentry family. After school in Białystok he studied at the Imperial Academy of Arts in St Petersburg, under Pavel Chistyakov and later in Ilya Repin's studio, alongside Polish students such as Ferdynand Ruszczyc. A study trip in 1893 took him to Beirut and Palestine, with stops in Constantinople, Greece and Egypt, and his diploma painting, Muhammad in the Desert, came out of that journey. He then trained in Paris at the Académie Julian, where he met Jean-Paul Laurens and Jean-Joseph Benjamin-Constant.

He showed work at the Paris World's Fair of 1900, in Munich in 1901 and in Venice in 1903. In 1902 he married the sculptor Julia Janiszewska, whom he had met at the St Petersburg Academy, and joined the Sztuka Society of Polish Artists. After settling in Warsaw he worked with Konrad Krzyżanowski on a private art school that led to the opening of the Warsaw School of Fine Arts in 1904. Stabrowski was its first director, and his best-known pupil there was the Lithuanian painter Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis.

Stabrowski was also a leading figure in Polish Theosophy and founded the first Theosophical lodges in Poland. His public interest in esotericism led to a conflict with the school's teaching council and supervising committee, and he left in 1909. From 1909 to 1913 he travelled through France, Germany, Sweden, Spain, Italy and the Canary Islands, and in the 1920s he visited Greece, Turkey, Morocco, Bosnia, Sweden and Norway. In 1922 he co-founded Sursum Corda, a group of artists with mystical interests.

In 1927 a solo exhibition at the Municipal Art Gallery in Łódź marked forty years of his work. He died in Garwolin, Poland, in 1929 and is buried at Powązki Cemetery in Warsaw. His paintings are held by the National Museum in Warsaw, the Lithuanian National Museum of Art, the Neue Pinakothek in Munich and the District Museum in Toruń.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Stabrowski is counted among the Symbolists. He painted scenes from fairy tales, legends and history, and symbolic, fantastic and mystical pictures shaped by his Theosophical beliefs, often with a decorative, Art Nouveau stylisation. In Paris he came into contact with Impressionism and Fauvism, and he took an interest in the philosophy of Henri Bergson. His landscapes, by contrast, are mostly realistic, and he also painted portraits and worked in applied art.

The collection shows each side of his work. Visionary pictures such as Consoler of monsters, Angel and monsters and the three Vision sketches for an Annunciation are built around bursts of light, while titles such as Fairy tale jewels and Princess of the magic crystal show his love of the fairy tale. Portraits include his wife, Julia Stabrowska, and the theatrical Portrait of Bronisław Brykner in fanciful costume. Travel works run from Taormina in 1901 to Kairouan in Tunisia, dated 1915 and 1926, and parks in Palermo and Rome dated 1924, with views of Sarajevo, Vilnius and a Norwegian fjord.

DECORATING WITH STABROWSKI PRINTS
========================================
Stabrowski's portraits are rich and dramatic. The peacock blues and stained-glass reds of the Zofia Borucińska portrait, or the black and gold of the Brykner portrait, suit a dining room, a hallway or a room with deep wall colours, where one picture can hold a wall on its own. His visionary pictures, in pale blues, creams and whites, are quieter and work well in a bedroom or a reading corner.

For warmth and light, the travel scenes bring the south indoors. View of Taormina, with its blue bay and a pot of red flowers, suits a sunny living room or kitchen, and Souq in Kairouan, with its white arches and robed figures, works well in a hallway or study. For a calmer, northern mood, Norwegian fjord and Boat at rocky coast fit a bathroom or a coastal-style bedroom.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Kazimierz Stabrowski part of?
A: Stabrowski was a Symbolist who combined mystical and fairy-tale subjects with decorative, Art Nouveau stylisation. He belonged to the Sztuka Society of Polish Artists and in 1922 co-founded the Sursum Corda group.

Q: Was Kazimierz Stabrowski Čiurlionis's teacher?
A: Yes. Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis is named as his best-known pupil at the Warsaw School of Fine Arts, which Stabrowski directed from 1904 to 1909.

Q: Where can I see Kazimierz Stabrowski's original works?
A: The National Museum in Warsaw holds several of his paintings, including Consoler of Monsters and Peacock – Portrait of Zofia Borucińska. His work is also in the Lithuanian National Museum of Art, the Neue Pinakothek in Munich and the District Museum in Toruń.

Q: How can I recognise Kazimierz Stabrowski's style?
A: His Symbolist works pair dreamlike, mystical subjects with decorative, Art Nouveau stylisation, while his landscapes are mostly realistic. In the collection, look for glowing light in the visionary pictures and richly patterned costume in the portraits.

Q: Is Kazimierz Stabrowski the same artist as Kazimieras Stabrauskas?
A: Yes. Kazimieras Stabrauskas is the Lithuanian form of his name, used in Lithuanian sources. He exhibited with the Lithuanian Art Society from 1907 to 1912.
