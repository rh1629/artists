========================================
ABOUT KAZIMIERZ STABROWSKI
========================================
Kazimierz Stabrowski was born in 1869 in Kruplany, then in the Russian Empire and now in Belarus, into a family of Polish landed gentry. He died in 1929 in Garwolin, near Warsaw. After school in Białystok he entered the Imperial Academy of Arts in St Petersburg in 1887. He was taught by Pavel Chistyakov and, from 1895, worked in the studio of Ilya Repin. His fellow students included Ferdynand Ruszczyc and Henryk Weyssenhoff.

In 1893 he travelled through Odessa, Constantinople, Greece and Egypt to Beirut and Palestine, gathering material for his diploma painting, Muhammad in the Desert (The Escape from Mecca). In the late 1890s he studied at the Académie Julian in Paris, where he came to know the academic painting of Jean-Joseph Benjamin-Constant and Jean-Paul Laurens, as well as Impressionism and Fauvism. He exhibited at the 1900 World's Fair in Paris, in Munich in 1901 and in Venice in 1903.

In 1902 he married the sculptor Julia Janiszewska, whom he had met at the academy, and joined the Society of Polish Artists "Sztuka". Settling in Warsaw, he ran a private art school with Konrad Krzyżanowski that led to the reopening of the Warsaw School of Fine Arts in 1904. Stabrowski was its first director, and his best-known pupil there was the Lithuanian painter Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis. He was also a leading Theosophist and founded the first Theosophical lodges in Poland. A conflict with the school's council over his esoteric activities led him to resign in 1909.

From 1909 to 1913 he travelled through France, Germany, Sweden, Spain, Italy and the Canary Islands, spending summers with his family at Lūznava Manor in Latvia, and he helped to organise early Lithuanian art exhibitions. He spent the First World War in Russia and returned to Warsaw in 1918. In the 1920s he travelled again in search of subjects, to Greece, Turkey, Morocco, Bosnia, Sweden and Norway, and in 1922 he co-founded the artists' group Sursum Corda. A solo exhibition in Łódź in 1927 marked 40 years of his work. He is buried at Powązki Cemetery in Warsaw, and his paintings are held by the National Museum in Warsaw, the Lithuanian National Museum of Art, the Bavarian State Painting Collections in Munich and the District Museum in Toruń.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Stabrowski was a Symbolist. He painted figure compositions drawn from fairy tales, legends and history, and his symbolic, fantastical and sometimes mystical scenes, shaped by his interest in Theosophy, are set out with the decorative patterns and stylised line of Art Nouveau. Light often seems to glow from inside his pictures. He also painted portraits, such as his 1908 portrait of Zofia Borucińska, known as Peacock, now in the National Museum in Warsaw, and landscapes from his travels, which are mostly more realistic in manner.

The collection covers both sides of his work. Fantastical pieces include Consoler of monsters, Angel and monsters, Fairy tale jewels, Princess of the magic crystal and three sketches for an Annunciation. Portraits include his wife Julia, Bronisław Brykner in fanciful costume and a Spanish woman in a pink dress. The travel scenes range from Taormina in 1901 and the parks of Palermo and Rome in 1924 to the streets, mosque and souq of Kairouan in Tunisia, Sarajevo, Vilnius and a Norwegian fjord.

DECORATING WITH STABROWSKI PRINTS
========================================
His Mediterranean landscapes, full of blue sea and sunlit greenery, suit a living room or a bright hallway. View of Taormina, with a vase of red flowers above a wide blue bay, makes a striking centrepiece, and Boat at rocky coast, in turquoise and cream, works well in a bathroom or a coastal-style bedroom. The 1924 park scenes from Palermo and Rome bring dappled shade to a reading corner.

For a study or dining room with deeper colours, the Symbolist works add atmosphere: the blues and reds of Consoler of monsters, or the misty greens of Crows – Council of Seniors. The warm stone and patterned arches of the Kairouan scenes look good together as a set in a hallway, and portraits such as Peacock, with its deep blue gown and stained glass, suit a bedroom or dressing area.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Kazimierz Stabrowski part of?
A: Stabrowski was a Symbolist whose work also draws on Art Nouveau. He was a member of the Society of Polish Artists "Sztuka" from 1902 and co-founded the group Sursum Corda in 1922.

Q: What is Kazimierz Stabrowski known for?
A: He is known for fantastical and mystical Symbolist paintings, portraits and travel landscapes. He was also the first director of the Warsaw School of Fine Arts, the teacher of Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis, and the founder of the first Theosophical lodges in Poland.

Q: Where can I see Kazimierz Stabrowski's original works?
A: The National Museum in Warsaw has the largest group, including Peacock, Consoler of Monsters and Lotuses in a Park in Palermo. His work is also held by the Lithuanian National Museum of Art, the Bavarian State Painting Collections in Munich and the District Museum in Toruń.

Q: How can I recognise Kazimierz Stabrowski's style?
A: Look for dreamlike figures, fairy-tale and mystical subjects, and light that seems to glow from within, framed by the flowing patterns of Art Nouveau. His travel landscapes are calmer and more realistic, with strong southern light.

Q: Is Kazimieras Stabrauskas the same artist as Kazimierz Stabrowski?
A: Yes. Kazimieras Stabrauskas is the Lithuanian form of his name, used by Lithuanian museums and reference works. He had close links with Lithuanian art through his pupil Čiurlionis and the early Lithuanian art exhibitions.
