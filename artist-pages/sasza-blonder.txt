========================================
ABOUT SASZA BLONDER
========================================
Sasza Blonder was born Aleksander (Szaje) Blonder on 27 May 1909 in Czortków, now Chortkiv in Ukraine. He grew up in a Jewish merchant family and spoke Polish, Hebrew and Yiddish. Drawn to Paris, he first went there in the late 1920s and studied architecture at the École nationale supérieure des beaux-arts. He then returned to Poland to study painting at the Academy of Fine Arts in Kraków under Teodor Axentowicz, Władysław Jarocki and Fryderyk Pautsch.

In Kraków he joined the city's left-wing avant-garde. He helped found the artists' association Żywi (The Living), which led to the Kraków Group, and he showed with the group from 1933 to 1937. His circle included Jonasz Stern, Maria Jarema, Leopold Lewicki, Henryk Wiciński and Stanisław Osostowicz. In 1935 he designed sets for the children's theatre of a Jewish school in Bielsko. In 1937 he became the first member of the Kraków Group to hold a solo exhibition, at the Koterba salon in Warsaw, where his original use of colour was noticed. The same year he was expelled from the Polish artists' union for his political activity.

Later in 1937 he settled in Paris, opened his own studio and took the name André Blondel. He met Montparnasse painters such as Chaïm Soutine and Pinchus Krémègne, and took part in the 1937 World Exhibition. When the Second World War began he joined the Polish army in France. After he was demobilised in Toulouse in 1940, he stayed in the south of France and was linked to the French Resistance, and his French name helped hide his Jewish identity. In 1943 he married Louise Bonfils, and they had a daughter, Hélène, and a son, Marc.

From 1944 to 1948 he lived between Carcassonne and Sète and worked intensely. He painted the harbour at Sète with François Desnoyer, made three portraits of the poet Joë Bousquet, and exhibited at the Salon des Indépendants, the Salon d'Automne and in towns across the south. In 1948 he moved with his family to Paris, where he died in an accidental fall in June 1949, aged 40. He left about 700 paintings and drawings. The National Museum in Warsaw, the National Museum in Kraków and the Musée des Beaux-Arts in Carcassonne hold his work.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Blonder's painting changed quickly over a short career, from colourism through abstraction to forms with a touch of surrealism. He painted mainly still lifes and landscapes, along with figure compositions, and he did not avoid Jewish themes. Marc Chagall, Chaïm Soutine, Amedeo Modigliani and Stanisław Wyspiański were strong influences. His Composition of about 1935 in the National Museum in Warsaw shows the mix: simple human shapes built from blocks and planes, with strong outlines and bright reds, pinks and yellows.

The collection follows him mostly through the 1930s. Works dated 1933 to 1935 include abstract and geometric compositions, a parade of figures, and still lifes of bottles, carafes, fruit and an inkwell. Works from 1936 and 1937 turn to portraits, a water-carrier at Krzemieniec, a peasant with a rake, country roads, mountain villages and landscapes with cows. Two later works show his years in France: the bright harbour of Boats at Collioure (1944) and Village Back-Street (1948).

DECORATING WITH BLONDER PRINTS
========================================
Blonder's landscapes suit everyday rooms. Landscape with a Red House, with its yellow fields and haystacks, brings warmth to a kitchen or dining room. For a calm bedroom or hallway, try his snowy Winter Landscape with Houses or the deep greens of Mountain Landscape with Cows. His still lifes of bottles, bread and fruit are a natural fit for a kitchen.

For more colour, Boats at Collioure and Village Back-Street work well in a living room or a bright hallway. The abstract compositions suit a modern living room, study or office. Kompozycja abstrakcyjna z czarnymi liniami (Abstract Composition with Black Lines), with its bold black strokes on pale grey, makes a strong single piece, while the geometric works from 1933 and 1934 look good hung together as a group.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Sasza Blonder part of?
A: He was a member of the Kraków Group, part of the Polish avant-garde of the 1930s, and showed in its exhibitions from 1933 to 1937. In France he is counted among the artists of the School of Paris.

Q: What is Sasza Blonder known for?
A: He is known for still lifes, landscapes and figure compositions that moved from colourism to abstraction and towards surrealism. He is also remembered as a Jewish artist of the Kraków avant-garde who rebuilt his career in France.

Q: Where can I see Sasza Blonder's original works?
A: The National Museum in Warsaw holds his Composition of about 1935. The National Museum in Kraków and the Musée des Beaux-Arts in Carcassonne also hold his work.

Q: Are Sasza Blonder and André Blondel the same artist?
A: Yes. André Blondel was the name Blonder used in France, and French sources usually call him by it. During the war the French name also helped hide his Jewish identity.

Q: How can I recognise Sasza Blonder's style?
A: Look for strong outlines, simplified forms and bold patches of colour, whether in a still life, a village street or an abstract composition. His figures are often reduced to schematic shapes built from blocks and planes.
