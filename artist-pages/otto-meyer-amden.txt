========================================
ABOUT OTTO MEYER-AMDEN
========================================
Otto Meyer-Amden was born Otto Meyer in Bern on 20 February 1885, the youngest of six children. His mother died in 1888, and after a time with foster parents he grew up in the orphanage in Bern, where he lived from 1892 to 1900. He went on to train as a lithographer in Zürich from 1903 to 1906, taking evening classes at the city's Kunstgewerbeschule, where he met the painter Hermann Huber in 1904. His work from these years includes symbolist watercolours.

In 1906 he moved to Munich to study at the Academy of Fine Arts, and from 1907 he studied at the Stuttgart Academy, where in 1909 he became a master student of Adolf Hölzel. In Stuttgart he became friends with Oskar Schlemmer, Willi Baumeister and Alfred Heinrich Pellegrini. Schlemmer remained a friend for life. On study trips to Strasbourg and Paris in 1907, he was deeply impressed by the paintings of Paul Cézanne.

In 1912 Baumeister and Huber invited him to join them in Amden, a village above Lake Walen in the canton of St Gallen. Both soon left, but Meyer stayed until 1928, living alone in an old farmhouse. He kept an intense diary, especially between 1913 and 1915, and read the Bible closely. From 1915 to 1918 he made dark-toned graphite drawings, and from 1918 he began working through memories of his orphanage youth in series such as Im Münster, Ess-Saal, Vorbereitung and Impfung. In 1922 he became friends with Ernst Ludwig Kirchner, and in 1923 he designed a round stained-glass window for the Zwinglihaus in Zürich-Wiedikon.

In 1928 he moved to Zürich to teach at the Kunstgewerbeschule, and in 1929 he showed twelve works in the exhibition Abstract and Surrealist Painting and Sculpture at the Kunsthaus Zürich. His last years were marked by serious illness, and he died in Zürich on 15 January 1933. He left only about 500 paintings and drawings and never made a living from his art, but he is now seen as an important forerunner of abstract painting in Switzerland. His work was shown at documenta in 1955 and 1964, and it is held by the Kunsthaus Zürich, the Kunstmuseum Bern and the Staatliche Kunsthalle Karlsruhe.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Meyer-Amden worked slowly and in series, returning to the same composition through many studies over several years. His pictures are mostly of figures, simplified and abstracted, with a lyrical, inward mood: boys in the orphanage at prayer, at table or being vaccinated, religious and symbolic scenes, studies of young men, street scenes in Zürich, and watercolours built from symbolic lines and geometric signs. His dark graphite drawings use a technique close to that of Georges Seurat. He never painted fully abstract pictures, but his ordered, reduced figures had a strong influence on Oskar Schlemmer.

The collection follows these series closely. There are many studies and overall compositions for Vorbereitung, his scene of morning prayers in the orphanage dining hall, and designs for the Zwinglihaus window. Studies of raised hands, diary pages and self-portraits sit alongside quieter works from Amden: a landscape study, starry skies over the lake and the mountains, and farmhouse rooms with figures at a table. Paradeplatz Zürich and Jüngling vor Strassenbahn show the city side of his work.

DECORATING WITH MEYER-AMDEN PRINTS
========================================
Meyer-Amden's Amden works suit calm, private rooms. Night pieces such as Starry Night above Lake Walen and Starry landscape with Mountain Peak bring a still, contemplative mood to a bedroom or reading corner, and the Landschaftsstudie Amden pairs well with them. For a dining room or kitchen, farmhouse interiors such as Mann und Frau am Tisch in Amdener Stube have a homely, everyday warmth.

His studies are made to be seen together. A group of Vorbereitung compositions or Zwinglihaus window designs, hung in a row in a hallway or study, shows how one idea grew over many years. For a home office or a city flat, Paradeplatz Zürich adds an urban note, and Paradiesgarten in Glaskugel, a paradise garden in a glass sphere, makes a small, curious centrepiece on its own.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Otto Meyer-Amden part of?
A: He belonged to no single movement. He trained under Adolf Hölzel in Stuttgart, and although he never painted fully abstract pictures, he is seen as an important forerunner of abstract painting in Switzerland. In 1929 he showed twelve works in an exhibition of abstract and surrealist art at the Kunsthaus Zürich.

Q: What is Otto Meyer-Amden known for?
A: He is known for figure compositions drawn from his youth in the Bern orphanage, above all the Vorbereitung series, and for studies of young men, Zürich street scenes and symbolic watercolours. He is also remembered for his influence on his friend Oskar Schlemmer.

Q: Where can I see Otto Meyer-Amden's original works?
A: The Kunsthaus Zürich and the Staatliche Kunsthalle Karlsruhe both hold versions of Vorbereitung, and the Kunstmuseum Bern also holds his work. The Kunstmuseum Basel staged a major exhibition of his work with Oskar Schlemmer in 2007 and 2008.

Q: Why is he called Meyer-Amden?
A: He was born Otto Meyer, and the name links him to Amden, the village above Lake Walen where he lived from 1912 to 1928. He signed his letters simply Otto Meyer.

Q: What makes Otto Meyer-Amden's style recognisable?
A: Look for simplified, quiet figures arranged in calm, ordered groups, often boys at prayer or at table, and for the same scene repeated across many studies. His drawings are dark and softly toned, and his subjects carry a lyrical, often religious mood.
