========================================
ABOUT OTTO PLATTNER
========================================
Otto Plattner was born in Liestal, in the canton of Basel-Landschaft, on 29 June 1886, the son of the merchant Wilhelm Plattner and Marie Plattner, née Strübin. He took courses at the Allgemeine Gewerbeschule in Basel, where the painter Fritz Schider supported him, and trained as a decorative painter at the École des Beaux-Arts in Geneva. In 1910 he worked in Paris with Hermenegildo Anglada Camarasa and in Munich with Moritz Heymann.

In 1911 he and his friend Burkhard Mangold restored Wilhelm Balmer's façade paintings on the Basel town hall, and the two painted their own faces into the work, behind bars. In 1912 he married Frida Lüdin, the daughter of a Liestal bookseller, and opened his own studio in Basel. Murals became a large part of his career: two frescoes for the main entrance of the Basel Zeughaus (1923–1925), house fronts in Basel such as the Haus zum Strytgärtli (1932), and paintings on the Oberes Tor and the town hall in Liestal.

Alongside this he designed posters, flags, coats of arms, festival cards and honorary certificates. His 1934 poster for the opening of the Liestal swimming pool, with its bold diagonal composition, has been called an icon of Swiss poster art. In 1930 he was one of the founders of the Kunstkredit Baselland, the canton's public art fund, and in 1936 he made his home in Liestal. He helped found the Basellandschaftliche Kunstvereinigung in 1944, and in 1947 he created the cantonal coat of arms of Basel-Landschaft.

He travelled to Italy, Tunis, Upper Bavaria and Hungary, and died in Stein am Rhein on 20 October 1951. He is buried in Liestal. His daughter Stefy Plattner gave most of his estate to the Dichter- und Stadtmuseum Liestal, and his 1920 portfolio Moderner Totentanz is held in the Sammlung Im Obersteg at the Kunstmuseum Basel.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
The Historical Dictionary of Switzerland describes Plattner as a patriotic painter. His subjects came from local and Swiss history, and he also painted portraits and landscapes of the Jura. His murals put historical figures and civic scenes on town gates and house fronts, and his heraldry, flags and festival cards drew on the same strong sense of place. He signed his work with a P and a small Swiss flag.

The collection is made up of his posters, dated in the store from 1914 to 1940. Many are vote and election posters for Basel and Basel-Landschaft, arguing for or against laws, initiatives and party lists. Others advertise events: cantonal shooting and singing festivals, Swiss National Day celebrations, masked balls at the Basel Stadtcasino, the landing of the Graf Zeppelin in Basel and the Liestal swimming pool. A few are commercial work, for a clearance sale, a bakery fair, a newspaper and a hard bread for children.

DECORATING WITH PLATTNER PRINTS
========================================
Plattner's posters were made to catch the eye in the street, so they hold their own on a large wall. Schwimmbad Liestal, with its blue sky and diving figure, suits a bathroom, a hallway or a bright living room. Graf Zeppelin – Landung Basel, with the airship over the city's red sandstone minster, is a good choice for a study or for anyone who loves travel and flight.

The masked ball posters, such as Quodlibet-Maskenball im Stadtcasino Basel and Casino-Maskenbälle 1934 Basel, bring a sense of fun to a dining room, a bar corner or an entrance hall. For a home office or library, a small group of the vote and festival posters makes a striking set of period pieces, especially for anyone with a link to Basel.

FAQ

Q: Who was Otto Plattner?
A: Otto Plattner (1886–1951) was a Swiss painter, graphic artist and heraldist from Liestal. He painted murals in Basel and Liestal and designed posters, flags and coats of arms, including the coat of arms of Basel-Landschaft in 1947.

Q: What is Otto Plattner known for?
A: He is known for his murals on town gates, town halls and house fronts, and for his posters. His 1934 poster for the Liestal swimming pool has been called an icon of Swiss poster art.

Q: Where can I see Otto Plattner's original works?
A: His murals can still be seen in Liestal and Basel, including the Oberes Tor in Liestal. Most of his estate is held by the Dichter- und Stadtmuseum Liestal, and his Moderner Totentanz portfolio is in the Sammlung Im Obersteg at the Kunstmuseum Basel.

Q: How can I recognise an Otto Plattner poster?
A: Look for Basel and Liestal subjects, Swiss flags and historical themes, and his signature: a P with a small Swiss flag.

Q: Who did Otto Plattner work with?
A: He was a close friend of the poster artist Burkhard Mangold, and the two restored the façade paintings of the Basel town hall together in 1911. Early in his training he was supported by the painter Fritz Schider.
