========================================
ABOUT OTTO PLATTNER
========================================
Otto Plattner was born in Liestal, the capital of the Swiss canton of Basel-Landschaft, on 29 June 1886. His father was a merchant. He took courses at the trade school in Basel, where the painter Fritz Schider encouraged him, and then trained as a decorative painter at the École des Beaux-Arts in Geneva. In 1910 he continued his studies in Paris with Hermen Anglada Camarasa and in Munich with Moritz Heymann.

In 1911 he worked with Burkhard Mangold to restore Wilhelm Balmer's painted façade of Basel Town Hall, and the two painted their own faces into it, peering out from behind bars. In 1912 he opened his own studio in Basel and married Frida Lüdin, a bookseller's daughter from Liestal. Alongside painting he designed posters, stained glass and book illustrations, and he became a sought-after painter of lanterns for the Basel carnival, the Fasnacht. He also drew political posters for the political right, campaigning in 1926 against unemployment benefits, public housing and compulsory unemployment insurance.

His wall paintings made him a public figure. He painted two frescoes for the entrance of the Basel arsenal between 1923 and 1925, and façades across Basel, Liestal, Laufen and Stein am Rhein. Study trips took him to Italy and Sicily in 1922 and to Tunis in 1926. In 1930 he helped found the Basel-Landschaft cantonal art fund, and in 1936 he moved back to Liestal. There he painted the large mural of the march to St. Jakob an der Birs in the town hall, and in 1947 he designed the canton's coat of arms.

Plattner died in Stein am Rhein on 20 October 1951 and is buried in Liestal. Thanks to a gift from his daughter Stefy, most of his estate is held by the Dichter- und Stadtmuseum in Liestal. His posters are in the Basel Poster Collection, and his 1920 portfolio A Modern Dance of Death is in the Im Obersteg Collection at the Kunstmuseum Basel.

========================================
STYLE AND SUBJECTS
========================================
Plattner took his subjects mainly from local and Swiss history, and he was known as a patriotic artist. Even his signature made the point: the letter P with a Swiss flag. Beyond his murals and posters he painted portraits, scenes of everyday life and landscapes of the Jura, and he designed flags, coats of arms, certificates, festival cards and stamps. His 1934 poster for Liestal's swimming pool, with its daring diagonal composition, has been called an icon of Swiss poster art.

The collection shows his poster work in full. Each design is built around one strong figure and large, clear lettering: a farmer scything wheat, a rifleman in a feathered hat, a diver in mid-air, a masked reveller at a carnival ball. There are posters for singing festivals, shooting contests, Swiss National Day and a bakers' trade fair, and many election and referendum posters from the 1920s and 1930s, with their sharp Basel humour.

DECORATING WITH PLATTNER PRINTS
========================================
Plattner's posters are made to be seen from across a room, so they work well as a single large print on a bare wall. Schwimmbad Liestal, all blue sky and red trunks, suits a bathroom, a hallway or a room with a summer feel. For a kitchen or dining room, try the food posters: Volro, the farmer in the golden field of the Kantonale Ausstellung landwirtschaftlicher Produkte des Baselbiets, or the leaping white stag of the Bäckerei- und Conditorei-Fachausstellung Basel.

The carnival ball posters bring colour and wit to a living room or a home bar, and they look good as a pair: hang Quodlibet-Maskenball next to Casino-Maskenbälle 1934 Basel. Graf Zeppelin suits a study or a child's room, and the election and referendum posters make a striking group for anyone interested in Swiss history.

FAQ
========================================
Q: What art movement was Otto Plattner part of?
A: Plattner was not part of a single movement. He was a Swiss poster artist and history painter who drew mainly on local and Swiss history, and he is remembered as a patriotic artist. He helped found the Basel-Landschaft cantonal art fund in 1930 and its art association in 1944.

Q: What is Otto Plattner known for?
A: He is best known for his posters and for his painted façades and murals in Liestal and Basel. His 1934 Schwimmbad Liestal poster has been called an icon of Swiss poster art, and he also designed the coat of arms of Basel-Landschaft in 1947.

Q: Where can I see Otto Plattner's original works?
A: Most of his estate is held by the Dichter- und Stadtmuseum in Liestal, and his posters are in the Basel Poster Collection and the Museum für Gestaltung in Zurich. In Liestal you can still see his murals on the Upper Gate and in the town hall.

Q: How can I recognise an Otto Plattner poster?
A: Look for his signature, the letter P with a small Swiss flag. His posters usually rest on one strong figure and large, clear lettering, and many use Swiss flags, historical costume and other patriotic symbols.

Q: Did Otto Plattner design the coat of arms of Basel-Landschaft?
A: Yes. In 1947 he drew the canton's coat of arms, replacing the Gothic ornaments on the bishop's staff with small balls. He also designed flags and arms for local groups in Liestal.
