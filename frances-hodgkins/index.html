Painter · New Zealand and Britain

Frances Hodgkins

b. 28 April 1869, Dunedin  ·  d. 13 May 1947, near Dorchester, Dorset

A watercolourist from Dunedin who left for Europe at 31 and spent four decades reinventing herself, ending up as one of the leading modernist painters in Britain.

About

Frances Mary Hodgkins grew up in Dunedin, the daughter of William Mathew Hodgkins, a lawyer and keen amateur watercolourist who gave her her first lessons. Her sister Isabel (later Isabel Field) also painted. Frances took classes with the visiting Italian painter G. P. Nerli in 1893 and studied at the Dunedin School of Art in 1895–96.

In 1901 she sailed for Europe. She lived there for the rest of her life apart from two return visits, moving between England, France, Morocco and Spain, painting and teaching to pay her way. Her early watercolours of people and markets gave way, from the late 1920s, to loose, high-keyed still lifes set in landscapes, painted in oil and later in gouache.

Recognition came late. She joined the Seven and Five Society in 1929, alongside younger artists such as Ben Nicholson and Barbara Hepworth, and in 1940 she was selected for the British pavilion at the Venice Biennale, a showing the war cancelled. She spent her last years in Corfe Castle, Dorset.

Legacy

Hodgkins is now counted among New Zealand's most important painters and as a significant figure in British modernism. The University of Otago's Frances Hodgkins Fellowship, set up in 1962, has supported generations of New Zealand artists.

For the 150th anniversary of her birth, Auckland Art Gallery staged Frances Hodgkins: European Journeys (4 May – 1 September 2019), curated by Mary Kisler after nearly ten years retracing her travels. It toured to Dunedin, Christchurch and Wellington into 2020. The gallery also publishes an online catalogue raisonné, The Complete Frances Hodgkins.
